# EXP-001 — Calibrate lexical evidence thresholds

## Question

Which cheap in-session evidence best predicts delayed recall and changed-context performance for near-A0 Nếp learners?

## Why this experiment exists

Literature supports differences among recognition, recall, cueing, and delayed performance, but does not supply a universal Nếp threshold or optimal delay.

## Initial design

Within each learner, distribute newly taught lexical targets across probe paths after equivalent initial teaching:

- A: recognition only;
- B: recognition + uncued/low-cue recall;
- C: recognition + recall + one controlled changed-context use.

Re-probe comparable targets after approximately 24 hours and again later if return behavior permits. Do not reveal answers before the scored attempt.

## Primary outcomes

- delayed meaning/form recall;
- changed-context comprehension/use;
- time-on-task per retained target.

## Secondary outcomes

- dropout/friction;
- number of hints requested;
- modality-specific failures (audio vs text).

## Decision rule

Choose the smallest evidence path that predicts later performance well enough to make curriculum/review decisions. Do not optimize for immediate accuracy alone.

## Unknowns to pre-register before running

- target sample size;
- exact delayed intervals;
- scoring tolerance for speech/spelling;
- learner inclusion criteria;
- minimum effect or prediction improvement worth the extra task time.
