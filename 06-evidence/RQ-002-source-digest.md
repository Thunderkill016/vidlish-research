# RQ-002 source digest — What Vietnamese scaffold helps without becoming the task?

## Scope

This digest asks whether a learner's first language (L1) can support L2 learning, which
forms of support have the strongest evidence, and what the literature **does not** tell us
about the exact amount or fading policy for Nếp.

The evidence below is not specific to Vietnamese learners unless noted. Nếp therefore uses
it to define candidate mechanisms and guardrails, then must validate the exact policy with
Vietnamese-speaking near-A0 adults.

## High-signal sources

### `SRC-0017` — Kim, Lee & Lee: L1 vs L2 gloss meta-analysis

- design: meta-analysis
- evidence base: 26 studies, 30 independent samples, 78 effect sizes, N=2,189
- outcomes: reading comprehension and vocabulary learning
- relevant finding: L1 glosses were more effective than L2 glosses overall; the advantage
  was substantially larger for beginner learners than for intermediate/higher learners.
- important limit: the advantage was clearer for vocabulary, especially immediate tests,
  than for reading comprehension; it does not provide a universal support-fading rule.
- Nếp relevance: concise Vietnamese meaning glosses are a defensible beginner scaffold.

### `SRC-0018` — Lee & Lee: teachers' verbal lexical explanation meta-analysis

- design: meta-analysis
- evidence base: 14 studies, 36 independent samples, N=3,304
- relevant finding: explicit lexical explanation improved vocabulary learning over no
  explanation, including delayed tests; L1 explanations outperformed L2 explanations.
- important limit: teacher-led classroom explanation is not identical to app UI support.
- Nếp relevance: forcing an A0 learner to decode an English-only definition can add
  unnecessary language difficulty when the target is the word's meaning.

### `SRC-0019` — Huang & Chalmers: pedagogical translanguaging systematic review

- design: systematic review with quality appraisal
- evidence base: 10 eligible EFL intervention studies
- relevant finding: evidence was mixed; several positive studies had high risk of bias and
  stronger causal claims were not justified.
- important limit: limited listening-specific and delayed evidence.
- Nếp relevance: this is a useful counterweight against treating translanguaging as
  automatically superior in every task.

### `SRC-0020` — Wang & Zhang: 2026 multilevel Bayesian meta-analysis

- design: multilevel Bayesian meta-analysis
- evidence base: 40 studies, 108 effect sizes, N=3,145
- relevant finding: pooled translanguaging-pedagogy effects on L2 achievement were positive,
  with substantial between-study heterogeneity.
- important limit: only three included studies used delayed posttests; long-term durability
  remains much less certain than immediate achievement.
- Nếp relevance: multilingual scaffolding is compatible with effective L2 learning, but the
  exact Nếp interaction still needs delayed testing.

### `SRC-0021` — Zhang & Brown: 2026 multilingual vs monolingual meta-analysis

- design: meta-analysis of learning outcomes
- evidence base: 24 studies, 30 samples, N=2,138
- relevant finding: multilingual approaches outperformed monolingual approaches on average;
  effects were reported as especially favorable for beginning-level learners.
- important limit: broad classroom interventions bundle many forms of L1 use, so the study
  does not tell Nếp whether a translation, hint, instruction or planning aid caused the gain.
- Nếp relevance: reject an English-only product dogma; isolate scaffold functions in product
  experiments.

### `SRC-0022` — de la Fuente & Goldenberg: principled L1 use with beginners

- design: semester-long beginner classroom intervention
- population: 54 university students learning elementary Spanish
- relevant finding: both L2-only and principled-L1 groups improved; the +L1 condition
  improved more in measured speaking and writing outcomes.
- important limit: small sample, Spanish target language, classroom setting.
- Nếp relevance: L1 support can coexist with productive L2 development rather than
  necessarily preventing it.

### `SRC-0023` — Brown: multilingual vs monolingual beginning French/Arabic

- design: 10-week quasi-experimental intervention, 25 hours of instruction
- population: beginning learners of French and Arabic
- relevant finding: multilingual groups generally outperformed monolingual groups across
  weekly progress and several skill measures.
- important limit: small/community-course contexts and non-English target languages.
- Nếp relevance: strengthens the case that beginners do not need an artificial
  target-language-only environment.

### `SRC-0024` — Mahdi, Mohsen & Almanea: second-order gloss meta-analysis

- design: second-order meta-analysis
- evidence base: seven prior meta-analyses covering 136 original studies
- relevant finding: glossing had a positive overall effect; beginner learners benefited
  strongly in the synthesized evidence.
- important limit: second-order analyses inherit overlap, design choices and measurement
  limitations from prior meta-analyses; recognition measures often produced larger effects.
- Nếp relevance: glosses are useful access tools but app success on recognition tasks must
  not be confused with independent language ability.

### `SRC-0025` — Yanagisawa, Webb & Uchihara: glossing meta-regression

- design: meta-analysis/meta-regression
- evidence base: 42 studies, 359 effect sizes, N=3,802
- relevant finding: glossed reading produced more vocabulary learning than nonglossed
  reading at immediate and delayed posttests; L1 glosses yielded greater gains than L2
  glosses in the pooled data.
- important limit: this is reading/vocabulary evidence, not direct evidence for listening,
  speaking, or whole-sentence translation.
- Nếp relevance: support the meaning of selected unknown lexical targets, not blanket
  translation of the entire English stream.

## Convergence

The strongest convergence is narrow:

1. banning L1 is not required for effective L2 learning;
2. L1 meaning support is often efficient and can be especially useful for beginners;
3. vocabulary gloss/explanation evidence is stronger than evidence for whole-course
   translanguaging policies;
4. support effects depend on the outcome and test format;
5. current research does not identify one universal percentage of L1 or a validated
   vocabulary-count threshold at which support should disappear.

## Key contradiction / uncertainty

`SRC-0019` found the causal EFL translanguaging intervention literature too weak and
heterogeneous for confident general claims, while the larger 2026 meta-analyses
(`SRC-0020`, `SRC-0021`) report positive pooled effects. This should be treated as
**increased support for strategic multilingual pedagogy, not proof that more L1 is always
better**.

The 2026 Bayesian meta-analysis also reports that only three included studies used delayed
posttests. Nếp therefore cannot infer long-term retention from immediate comprehension or
achievement effects.

## Evidence boundary for RQ-002

This cycle does **not** establish:

- an optimal percentage of Vietnamese text/audio in a session;
- that Vietnamese should always be visible;
- that full sentence translation is superior to a micro-gloss;
- that support should disappear after 30, 300, or 1,000 words;
- that L1 support during a scored task measures independent English ability;
- a Vietnamese-specific causal effect.

Those are product questions for `EXP-002` and later Vietnamese-targeted studies.
