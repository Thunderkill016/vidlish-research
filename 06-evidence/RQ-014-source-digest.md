# RQ-014 source digest

## Research question

How should Vietnamese near-A0 learners progress from controlled written production to independent meaningful writing, with feedback that improves future writing rather than merely correcting the current text?

## Planning and writing processes

### SRC-0144 — Johnson & Abdi Tabari (2023)
Research synthesis and meta-analysis on planning in L2 writing. Planning can affect dimensions such as fluency and complexity, but effects are not uniform across writing outcomes. Useful for treating planning as one process in writing rather than a universal quality booster.

### SRC-0145 — Ellis (2021)
Systematic review of 32 experimental studies on pre-task planning in L2 writing. Pre-task planning generally improved fluency, while evidence for overall rated quality and complexity was inconsistent; online planning can sometimes support accuracy. This argues for preserving planning, formulation and editing as distinct processes.

## Written corrective feedback

### SRC-0146 — Brown, Liu & Norouzian (2023)
Bayesian meta-analysis of written corrective feedback (WCF) using an initial pool of 52 controlled primary studies. It reports robust evidence of moderate effectiveness on L2 written accuracy over time and does not support one universally superior feedback type across direct, indirect and metalinguistic feedback.

### SRC-0147 — Kang (2022)
Meta-analysis of 25 studies reporting both immediate and delayed WCF effects. WCF improved grammatical accuracy relative to no-WCF conditions, with smaller effects at delayed measurement. Writing genre and feedback type moderated effects; longer-term evidence remains limited.

The product implication is not “correct every error.” It is to test whether accuracy improves in later, independently written text.

## Peer feedback

### SRC-0148 — Vuogan & Li (2022/2023)
Meta-analysis of 26 L2 peer-feedback studies. Peer feedback had a positive overall effect on writing, but effects were larger for revisions than for new compositions and stronger for content than for language. Comparisons with teacher feedback and self-revision remain tentative because the relevant primary-study pool is small.

This is a direct warning against using successful revision as proof of independent writing development.

## Automated and AI-mediated feedback

### SRC-0149 — Fleckenstein, Liebenow & Meyer (2023)
Multilevel meta-analysis of automated writing evaluation (AWE) feedback across 20 studies / 84 effects. Automated feedback had a medium overall effect with substantial heterogeneity. Importantly, the review distinguishes revision performance from transfer to new writing and warns against treating AWE as one homogeneous intervention.

### SRC-0150 — Ngo, Chen & Lai (2022)
Three-level meta-analysis focused on EFL/ESL AWE. Between-group effects were moderate overall and varied by outcome, duration, platform and learner/context characteristics. Medium-to-long usage tended to outperform very short use.

### SRC-0155 — Tseng, Chen & Dong (2026)
Meta-synthesis of eleven systematic reviews/meta-analyses on AWE research. The synthesis supports AWE as potentially useful, especially for surface-level writing such as grammar/mechanics, while highlighting weaker evidence for higher-order writing and risks around reliability and over-reliance. It recommends AWE as an adjunct rather than a replacement for human/pedagogical support.

For Nếp, AI-generated polished text must never be counted as learner writing evidence.

## Self-regulated writing and feedback use

### SRC-0151 — Teng & Zhang (2019/2020)
Five-month L2 writing intervention based on self-regulated learning strategies. The treatment group improved writing performance at post- and delayed post-test and reported greater use of self-regulated strategies and self-efficacy. Population was university EFL, so exact near-A0 sequencing remains unproven.

### SRC-0152 — Yang, Liu & Xu (2022)
Semester-long EFL study comparing conventional task-level feedback with feedback targeting process/self-regulation plus supplementary activities. The treatment supported writing performance and several self-regulated writing strategies, with analytic gains varying across content, organization, vocabulary and language use.

These sources support teaching learners to plan, monitor, interpret feedback and revise rather than making feedback a passive correction service.

## Model texts and positive evidence

### SRC-0153 — Wu, Qie & Wang (2023)
Four-stage EFL writing study using model texts after an initial draft. Learners compared their own text with models, rewrote, and later produced a delayed composition. Models supported noticing and improvements in several writing dimensions; effects varied by learner analytic ability and guidance.

The sequencing is important:

```text
learner writes first
→ model appears second
→ compare/noticing
→ model disappears
→ learner writes again
```

This protects learner ownership better than exposing a perfect model before the first attempt.

### SRC-0154 — Roothooft, Lázaro-Ibarrola & Bulté (2022/2025)
Study with young EFL writers comparing task repetition, direct corrections and model-text feedback. Direct correction mainly improved accuracy, while models supported lexical diversity/text quality; task repetition alone produced only small complexity changes. Population is children, so it is supporting evidence rather than a direct adult near-A0 policy.

## Main evidence gap

The literature does not establish one optimal writing ladder for Vietnamese literate adults near A0, nor exact rules for:

- when sentence frames should disappear;
- how many sentences a beginner should generate independently;
- whether feedback should prioritize message, vocabulary, construction or mechanics at each stage;
- when direct correction should replace a retrieval prompt;
- when model texts should be shown;
- how much AI/AWE support can be used without masking ability;
- how much revision is useful before learners should move to a new task;
- how to weight content, organization, accuracy and fluency in very short beginner writing.

Those parameters belong to `EXP-014`.
