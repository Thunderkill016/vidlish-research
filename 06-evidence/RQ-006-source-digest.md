# RQ-006 source digest — controlled speaking near A0

## Research question

Which speaking tasks are valid and useful for Vietnamese-speaking adults near A0, and how should Nếp move from supported short production toward guided interaction without pretending repetition or script-reading is spontaneous speaking?

## Working distinction

For this cycle:

- **rehearsal** = repeating, shadowing, reading aloud, or speaking while the answer/model remains visible;
- **controlled production** = the learner must retrieve a bounded target orally, with the response space constrained enough to isolate the intended language;
- **guided interaction** = the learner must produce a relevant turn in response to a standardized interlocutor or situation;
- **open production** = the learner chooses much of the content and language with few constraints;
- **independent speaking evidence** = answer-bearing language is not visible at response time and support provenance is recorded.

These are Nếp task/evidence labels, not claims that speaking consists of independent psychological modules.

## Source set and extraction

### `SRC-0001` — CEFR Companion Volume

- The CEFR explicitly adds a Pre-A1 band below A1.
- It characterizes Pre-A1 as a stage where learners have not yet developed broad generative capacity and rely heavily on words and formulaic expressions.
- Overall spoken production at Pre-A1 includes short phrases giving basic personal information.
- Overall spoken interaction includes asking/answering simple questions about self and routines with short formulaic expressions and gestures.

**Use for Nếp:** free conversation is not a sensible default baseline for near-A0. Short meaningful utterances and tightly bounded interaction are legitimate early communicative targets.

**Scope limit:** CEFR is descriptive, not a causal teaching algorithm.

### `SRC-0054` — Shintani, Li & Ellis, comprehension- vs production-based instruction meta-analysis

- Synthesized 35 research projects in 30 published studies directly comparing comprehension- and production-based grammar instruction.
- Both approaches produced substantial learning.
- Production-based instruction showed a relative advantage for delayed productive knowledge, while comprehension-based instruction had an early receptive advantage.

**Use for Nếp:** if the desired capability is oral production, the curriculum eventually needs actual production practice; comprehension success should not be expected to automatically become speaking skill.

**Scope limit:** grammar-instruction meta-analysis, not specifically A0 speaking or Vietnamese learners.

### `SRC-0055` — Lee & Kim, low-proficiency oral planning study

- 168 low-proficiency Korean EFL college learners received the same instruction but different planning support before speaking.
- Learners who first wrote what they wanted to say outperformed learners who only read model answers on speaking scores and fluency measures.
- The study directly challenges the assumption that output-based preparation is inherently unsuitable for low-level learners.

**Use for Nếp:** near-A0 learners can be prepared for oral output with bounded productive planning; planning need not be limited to passive model exposure.

**Scope limit:** planning through writing adds literacy demands and may not fit every near-A0 learner; the study does not establish Nếp's ideal support format.

### `SRC-0056` — Johnson & Abdi Tabari, task-planning research synthesis/meta-analysis

- Synthesizes the oral L2 planning literature and reports clear overall effects of planning on subsequent oral production.
- Planning influences complexity, accuracy, lexical complexity and/or fluency differently depending on implementation and task conditions.

**Use for Nếp:** speaking difficulty can be reduced through planned support before the attempt, but the type and amount of preparation must be recorded because it changes the performance being observed.

### `SRC-0057` — Kim, novice learner oral task sequencing

- 76 novice university EFL learners completed sequenced oral-task interventions.
- Guided planning with vocabulary improved speed fluency and reduced dysfluency relative to content-only planning.
- Complexity-focused sequencing could improve complexity while creating accuracy/dysfluency trade-offs.

**Use for Nếp:** a speaking ladder should control task complexity and give lexical/chunk support before expecting more demanding oral performance. More complex output is not automatically better beginner practice.

### `SRC-0058` — Abdi Tabari, Zhuang & Farahanynia, task-repetition meta-analysis

- Meta-analysis of task repetition in L2 oral production found positive effects across oral performance dimensions, with the largest pattern for syntactic complexity/accuracy and smaller positive effects for fluency/lexical complexity.
- Effects varied by task type, repetition type, learning context, spacing and learner proficiency.

**Use for Nếp:** repeating a speaking task can support proceduralization and refinement, but repetition is a practice mechanism rather than proof of spontaneous or transferred speaking ability.

### `SRC-0059` — van Batenburg et al., standardized interactive speech tasks

- Six scripted interaction tasks standardized interlocutor contributions while eliciting instruction, advice and sales interactions.
- Ratings showed strong inter-rater agreement and differentiated interactional performance.
- Analytic features such as meaning negotiation and correcting misinterpretation added information beyond a single holistic score.

**Use for Nếp:** guided role-play/Q&A with a standardized interlocutor can provide meaningful interactional evidence without requiring completely open conversation.

**Scope limit:** pre-vocational learners, not a Vietnamese Pre-A1 validation study.

### `SRC-0060` — Lyster & Saito, oral corrective feedback meta-analysis

- Meta-analysis of 15 classroom studies (N = 827) found significant and durable effects of oral corrective feedback on L2 development.
- Prompts had larger effects than recasts in some contrasts, although feedback effects varied by design and learner factors.
- Productive/constructed-response outcomes were important for observing learning.

**Use for Nếp:** after an oral attempt, a prompt that gives the learner a chance to self-repair is a defensible first feedback move when the target is plausibly retrievable; model reveal remains available when repair fails.

**Scope limit:** not a direct comparison of Nếp-style automated feedback, and no universal feedback sequence is established.

### `SRC-0061` — Boers & Lindstromberg, formulaic-sequence intervention review

- Reviews experimental/intervention research on noticing, looking up and memorizing formulaic sequences in L2 learning.
- Supports the value of building a repertoire of multiword sequences, while showing that instructional effects depend on treatment and outcome.

**Use for Nếp:** chunks/formulaic expressions are reasonable units for early speaking practice, consistent with the CEFR Pre-A1 profile.

**Scope limit:** evidence spans varied proficiency levels and does not establish a specific A0 chunk list or speaking sequence.

## Evidence boundaries

This cycle did **not** establish:

- that repeating a model is independent speaking ability;
- that reading a prepared script aloud demonstrates spontaneous production;
- that every learner should write before speaking;
- that one task-complexity sequence is universally optimal;
- that more open conversation is always better practice;
- that one fluency metric is a valid global speaking score;
- that one standardized role-play captures all interactional competence;
- that ASR can validly score Vietnamese near-A0 production;
- a pronunciation threshold for successful speaking;
- the exact support-fading rule from controlled production to free interaction.

The final three boundaries remain explicit dependencies of `RQ-007`, `RQ-008` and `EXP-006`.
