# RQ-012 source digest

## Research question

How should near-A0 learners learn and demonstrate grammar/construction knowledge without reducing grammar to rule memorization and decontextualized drills?

## High-level instruction evidence

### SRC-0119 — Norris & Ortega (2000)
Seminal quantitative synthesis of instructed L2 research. Focused instruction produces substantial target-oriented gains and explicit treatments often outperform implicit treatments. Critical limitation for Nếp: many outcome measures are constrained and favor explicit/controlled knowledge; free-response outcomes are a different evidential target.

### SRC-0120 — Spada & Tomita (2010)
Meta-analysis of explicit versus implicit instruction for simple and complex grammatical features. Explicit instruction showed advantages across feature complexity, including effects on production measures, but the construct represented by each outcome measure remains important.

### SRC-0121 — Kang, Sok & Han (2019)
Thirty-five-year meta-analysis of form-focused instruction. Reinforces that instruction matters while showing moderation by research design/timing and cautioning against one-method conclusions.

### SRC-0122 — Goo, Granena, Yilmaz & Novella (2015)
Updated meta-analytic comparison of explicit and implicit instruction. Explicit instruction generally produced larger effects, but effect size varies by timing and outcome measure. This source is especially useful for separating short-term rule/test gains from broader use.

## Input processing versus output

### SRC-0123 — Shintani, Li & Ellis (2013)
Meta-analysis comparing comprehension-based and production-based grammar instruction. Both produced large receptive and productive gains. Comprehension-based instruction had an early receptive advantage; production-based instruction showed an advantage on delayed productive knowledge. The early comprehension advantage was largely associated with Processing Instruction.

### SRC-0124 — Shintani (2015)
Meta-analysis of Processing Instruction (PI) versus production-based instruction. PI was stronger for receptive knowledge; production-based instruction was comparable or stronger for productive outcomes under some conditions. Explicit information was not the sole cause of PI effects.

### SRC-0125 — Lee & Huang (2008)
Meta-analytic review of visual/textual input enhancement. Merely making a grammatical form visually salient produces only small/inconsistent learning effects. Highlighting is not a grammar curriculum.

## Usage, constructions and form–meaning mapping

### SRC-0126 — Ellis (2022)
Usage-based synthesis of L2 morphology. Grammar can be represented as constructions: learned form–function/form–meaning pairings shaped by frequency, contingency, salience, formulaic contexts and L1-tuned attention. Explicit form focus may help learners notice weak mappings, but subsequent meaningful usage is required for entrenchment and fluent access.

### SRC-0131 — Hwang (2023)
Controlled EFL study of caused-motion constructions. Instruction balancing attention to construction form and core meaning outperformed metalanguage-only and input-flood conditions on the study's measures, including generalization. Useful direct evidence for meaning-linked construction instruction, but narrow in target construction and population.

## Measurement of grammar knowledge

### SRC-0127 — Kim & Godfroid (2023)
Longitudinal study of explicit/implicit L2 knowledge and their interface. Shows the constructs are related but distinct and that measurement is difficult. Rule/metalinguistic performance cannot stand alone as evidence of fluent grammar use.

### SRC-0128 — Suzuki & DeKeyser (2015)
Validation work shows elicited imitation can draw on automatized explicit knowledge rather than pure implicit knowledge; real-time meaning-focused measures add different evidence. Important warning against labeling one grammar test as `implicit_mastery`.

## Corrective feedback

### SRC-0129 — Lyster & Saito (2010)
Classroom meta-analysis found durable benefits from oral corrective feedback; prompts showed larger effects than recasts in that synthesis and effects were especially apparent on free constructed response measures.

### SRC-0130 — Li (2010)
Broader corrective-feedback meta-analysis found a medium durable overall effect and moderator differences, including evidence that implicit feedback effects could be maintained well over time. Together with SRC-0129, this argues against one universal feedback type.

## Evidence gap

The literature supports instruction, form–meaning processing, meaningful production and corrective feedback, but it does **not** establish one optimal near-A0 sequence for Vietnamese adults or a universal rule-explanation dose.

Nếp therefore needs `EXP-012` to compare an integrated construction loop with simpler rule-first and exposure-only alternatives using delayed unseen interpretation and production outcomes.
