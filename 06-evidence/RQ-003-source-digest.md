# RQ-003 source digest — Separating perception from comprehension

## Scope

This cycle asks how Nếp can tell whether a listening failure comes from failure to perceive/decode the speech signal, failure to access known lexical meaning, or failure to construct the message. It focuses on EFL/ESL listening, especially lower-proficiency learners, and does not assume that one diagnostic task measures the whole listening construct.

## High-signal sources

### `SRC-0026` — Jia & Hew (2021), decoding-training systematic review/meta-analysis

- 33 empirical studies reviewed; 13 intervention studies meta-analyzed.
- Decoding training targeted recognition/segmentation of speech; dictation and pronunciation instruction were common activities.
- The meta-analysis reported a significant medium benefit over non-decoding instruction (Hedges' g = 0.553, 95% CI [0.348, 0.759]).
- Limit: interventions and outcome measures varied; decoding gains are not identical to message comprehension.

### `SRC-0027` — Chang (2024), listening-instruction meta-analysis

- 32 primary studies, 39 samples, N=2,797.
- Listening instruction had a small positive pooled effect (d = 0.25); longer treatment was associated with larger effects.
- Limit: broad listening-instruction category; does not prescribe one micro-task sequence for A0.

### `SRC-0028` — Dalman & Plonsky, listening-strategy meta-analysis

- 45 primary studies / 51 independent samples.
- Listening strategy instruction showed a positive pooled effect, with moderator-dependent variation.
- Limit: strategy instruction does not replace signal-level decoding practice and many studies target learners above true A0.

### `SRC-0029` — Uchihara, Karas & Thomson (2025), HVPT meta-analysis

- 79 studies of high-variability phonetic training.
- Medium-to-large gains in L2 speech perception, with evidence of retention and some generalization to novel stimuli.
- Limit: phonetic perception is a component of listening, not proof of message-level comprehension.

### `SRC-0030` — Carney (2020/2021), diagnosing known lexis

- 15 Japanese EFL learners across proficiency levels.
- L1 recall + L2 repetition diagnosed cases where orthographically known words were persistently misheard.
- Reported causes included L1 phonological influence, connected-speech modifications and misleading top-down interpretation.
- Limit: small diagnostic study; useful for mechanism and task design rather than effect-size claims.

### `SRC-0031` — Saito et al. (2025), phonological vocabulary and listening

- 126 Japanese EFL listeners.
- Phonological vocabulary factors made a large contribution to variation in listening proficiency; automatized contextual access was especially important, with phonologization also important for low-to-mid proficiency.
- Limit: correlational/predictive study and Japanese population; does not establish a causal training recipe for Vietnamese learners.

### `SRC-0032` — Matthews & Cheng (2015), word recognition from speech

- 167 tertiary Chinese EFL learners.
- Recognition of high-frequency words from connected speech correlated strongly with listening comprehension; one frequency-band WRS measure predicted a large share of listening-score variance.
- Limit: prediction is not identity or causation; partial dictation includes written-response demands.

### `SRC-0033` — Leonard (2019), decoding vs comprehension

- 25 intermediate-to-advanced L2 Spanish listeners.
- Aural decoding and comprehension were strongly related, but good comprehension sometimes occurred with imperfect word-by-word decoding.
- Limit: small sample, non-English target language, higher proficiency than Nếp Stage 0.
- Nếp relevance: do not make perfect transcription a prerequisite for understanding a message.

### `SRC-0034` — He & Jiang (2020), listening-assessment review

- 87 studies from 2001–2020 reviewed in a socio-cognitive framework.
- Listening assessment validity depends on task setting, input/output demands, speakers and the cognitive processes elicited.
- The review describes lower-level input decoding/lexical search/parsing and higher-level meaning/discourse construction as distinguishable processing levels.
- Nếp relevance: a task must be labeled by the process it actually elicits.

### `SRC-0035` — Yuan & Tang (2025), low-proficiency listening intervention

- 80 low-proficiency Chinese EFL college learners over 10 weeks.
- In this study, combined metacognitive + bottom-up instruction produced the strongest listening improvement, followed by bottom-up instruction; metacognitive instruction alone was not useful in that sample.
- Limit: one small study; supports integration as a candidate, not a universal ranking.

### `SRC-0036` — Wong et al. (2021), connected-speech perceptual errors

- 60 Chinese ESL undergraduates; 640 connected-speech dictation errors classified.
- Errors commonly involved vowel/consonant perception, syllable-number changes and function-word substitutions.
- Limit: diagnostic error taxonomy, not an intervention trial.

## Convergence

The research converges on a practical distinction:

```text
speech signal
→ phonetic/perceptual discrimination
→ segmentation + spoken-word recognition
→ lexical/syntactic access
→ proposition/message construction
→ discourse/inference/monitoring
```

Breakdown can occur at more than one stage. A learner who fails a meaning question has not automatically demonstrated a vocabulary problem, and a learner who fails transcription has not automatically failed to understand the message.

## Evidence boundary

This cycle does **not** establish:

- a universal word-decoding percentage required for comprehension;
- one optimal replay count;
- that dictation is a pure listening measure;
- that phoneme drills alone create listening comprehension;
- that a transcript should never be shown during teaching;
- a Vietnamese-specific list of decoding errors.

Those remain product or population-specific questions.
