# Product principles

| ID | Principle | Confidence | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PRN-001` | Measure capability, not completion. | high | Learning claims require task evidence. |
| `PRN-002` | Record comprehension, recall, transfer and retention separately as task/time evidence; do not claim they are independent latent abilities. | high | Measurement claims must stay narrower than psychological construct claims. |
| `PRN-003` | Attempt before reveal when practicing retrieval. | high | Visible answers convert retrieval into recognition/review. |
| `PRN-004` | Use bounded, understandable input before broad free output near A0. | moderate | Beginners need language to work with; controlled output can still begin early. |
| `PRN-005` | Space review, but do not equate scheduler state with mastery. | high | Spacing supports memory; schedule metadata is not a performance. |
| `PRN-006` | Treat captions and Vietnamese translation as scaffolds that can be faded. | moderate | Support can aid comprehension but can also replace the intended task. |
| `PRN-007` | Prefer intelligibility over native-accent imitation. | moderate | Product goal is successful communication, not accent conformity. |
| `PRN-008` | AI proposes; evidence and product gates decide. | moderate | Generation reliability and assessment validity are separate problems. |
| `PRN-009` | Authentic video is a graduated source, not proof of a beginner curriculum. | moderate | Difficulty and coverage must match learner capability. |
| `PRN-010` | Product assumptions need experiments even when mechanisms are plausible. | high | Educational evidence does not determine exact UX. |
| `PRN-011` | Do not use one irreversible `known/mastered` boolean as the sole lexical state. | high | Lexical performance is incremental and task-sensitive. |
| `PRN-012` | Match learner-state claims to the elicitation task and support level. | high | Recognition, recall and cued production support different inferences. |
| `PRN-013` | Require delayed re-demonstration before calling lexical evidence retained. | high | Immediate performance overstates durable retention. |
| `PRN-014` | Treat useful chunks as lexical targets and record phrase-level cues/support. | moderate | Chunk measurement has distinct receptive/productive and cueing issues. |
| `PRN-015` | Near A0, prioritize useful form-meaning and spoken/written form first; deepen lexical use progressively. | moderate | Total word-knowledge mastery is not a realistic prerequisite for progression. |
| `PRN-016` | Near A0, allow strategic Vietnamese support; do not enforce English-only as a product virtue. | high | Multilingual/L1-inclusive instruction can support L2 learning, especially at beginner levels. |
| `PRN-017` | Use Vietnamese to reduce unnecessary access cost while keeping English form/audio/context as the learning object. | high | L1 lexical support can be efficient without replacing target-language practice. |
| `PRN-018` | Hide answer-bearing Vietnamese during independent probes and record revealed support. | high | Supported success and independent English performance are different inferences. |
| `PRN-019` | Prefer progressive disclosure of the smallest useful scaffold over permanently visible full translation. | moderate | Evidence supports scaffolding, not blanket translation or a universal amount. |
| `PRN-020` | Fade Vietnamese from demonstrated lower-support performance, not a fixed day or vocabulary count. | moderate | Lower proficiency often benefits more, but exact fading thresholds are unvalidated. |
| `PRN-021` | Keep instruction, lexical gloss, context hint, contrastive note, feedback and full translation as distinct scaffold types. | moderate | Multilingual interventions bundle different mechanisms; product data must not. |
| `PRN-022` | Judge scaffolds by later unsupported English performance plus task access, not immediate ease alone. | high | Delayed evidence is limited and recognition-heavy outcomes can overstate durable learning. |
| `PRN-023` | Separate speech perception/decoding evidence from message-comprehension evidence. | high | Listening breakdown can occur at distinguishable processing levels. |
| `PRN-024` | Capture an audio-only attempt before answer-bearing transcript/captions when measuring independent listening. | high | Written support changes what the task demonstrates. |
| `PRN-025` | Diagnose the critical decoding/form-meaning step after short-message failure instead of only replay/reveal. | moderate | Known written language may remain inaccessible in speech. |
| `PRN-026` | Record speaker, replay count, transcript visibility and support provenance for listening attempts. | high | Listening evidence depends on input and support conditions. |
| `PRN-027` | Do not use strict full dictation as the sole beginner decoding measure. | moderate | Dictation can mix decoding with spelling/written production. |
| `PRN-028` | Train known targets in connected speech and later vary speakers/phonetic contexts. | high | Spoken-word recognition and perception must generalize beyond one recording. |
| `PRN-029` | Integrate bottom-up work with message comprehension and metacognitive feedback. | moderate | Neither perception drills nor strategy instruction alone represent the full listening process. |
| `PRN-030` | Test listening transfer with unseen parallel utterances and, when feasible, new speakers. | high | Replaying a trained recording cannot establish generalized listening ability. |
| `PRN-031` | Use retrieval only after enough initial access for a meaningful attempt; repeated failure is not desirable difficulty. | high | Retrieval benefits depend on successful enough retrieval. |
| `PRN-032` | Optimize review for delayed unsupported performance per minute, not repetition count. | high | More within-session retrieval can help but consumes learner time. |
| `PRN-033` | Prefer adaptive spacing over a universal interval ritual, with a simple fallback for sparse data. | high | Spacing works, but exact optimal intervals vary. |
| `PRN-034` | Do not treat expanding versus equal spacing as a product doctrine. | high | Meta-analysis finds no universal superiority of expanding spacing. |
| `PRN-035` | Match review task to the capability evidence needed. | high | Recognition, recall, aural access and transfer support different inferences. |
| `PRN-036` | Capture retrieval before answer feedback, then correct efficiently rather than imposing a universal feedback delay. | high | L2 feedback-timing evidence does not support one mandatory delay. |
| `PRN-037` | Keep scheduler state and language-capability evidence as separate data models. | high | Recall prediction/due timing is not mastery. |
| `PRN-038` | Derive scheduler outcomes from observable attempts/support provenance; treat FSRS grade mapping as product calibration. | moderate | Scheduler labels are not validated language-assessment categories. |
| `PRN-039` | Calibrate desired retention against learning gain, review workload and backlog. | moderate | Higher retention has a cost; the Nếp optimum is unvalidated. |
| `PRN-040` | Exact trained-context retests are retention evidence, not transfer. | high | Transfer requires a meaningful change in task/cue/context. |
| `PRN-041` | Near A0, begin transfer with unseen parallel items and one bounded change. | high | Too much novelty can turn a transfer probe into overload. |
| `PRN-042` | Increase context diversity from demonstrated capability rather than maximizing variation. | high | L2 benefits of contextual diversity are proficiency- and task-dependent. |
| `PRN-043` | Keep non-target language and interface demands inside a controlled novelty budget. | high | Transfer failure should be attributable to the intended target as narrowly as possible. |
| `PRN-044` | Keep comprehension/recognition/controlled-production/open-production transfer evidence distinct. | high | Different response demands support different inferences. |
| `PRN-045` | Productive transfer requires a productive changed-context attempt. | high | Receptive success cannot certify independent production. |
| `PRN-046` | Use constrained production near A0 when it isolates the target; do not require free conversation for every probe. | moderate | Free output adds many confounds and substantial learner cost. |
| `PRN-047` | Record which context, speaker, modality, cue and response dimensions changed. | high | “Transfer” without a defined change is uninterpretable. |
| `PRN-048` | Preserve first-seen provenance for transfer probes. | high | Once attempted, a probe becomes learning exposure and is no longer unseen. |
| `PRN-049` | Sample transfer according to evidence need and time budget, then calibrate against later unseen performance. | moderate | Exact probe frequency and novelty thresholds are product assumptions. |
| `PRN-050` | Start meaningful speaking near A0 with short bounded production; do not make open conversation the gate to having a speaking curriculum. | high | Pre-A1 speaking is narrow/formulaic and productive skill needs productive practice. |
| `PRN-051` | Progress speaking demand from rehearsal to independent retrieval, controlled recombination, guided interaction and only later sampled open interaction. | moderate | Novice task demand should increase progressively rather than jump to unrestricted production. |
| `PRN-052` | Store listen-and-repeat, shadowing and read-aloud as rehearsal evidence; never silently promote model-visible performance to independent speaking evidence. | high | Support present at response time changes what the task demonstrates. |
| `PRN-053` | Allow planning and lexical/chunk support to reduce beginner speaking load, but hide answer-bearing language before an independent attempt and record planning/support provenance. | high | Planning helps performance while also changing the evidential conditions. |
| `PRN-054` | Match each speaking claim to its response demand: a productive claim requires oral retrieval and an interaction claim requires a contingent response to another turn. | high | Speaking constructs depend on elicitation demands. |
| `PRN-055` | Use standardized guided Q&A, information-gap or role-play tasks to introduce interaction before unrestricted AI conversation becomes the primary beginner measure. | moderate | Bounded interactive tasks can elicit diagnosable interactional behavior. |
| `PRN-056` | Use oral task repetition to rehearse and proceduralize performance, but require a changed or unseen task for fresh speaking-transfer evidence. | high | Same-task gains are not transfer. |
| `PRN-057` | Increase speaking difficulty progressively and avoid stacking multiple new difficulty sources at once for near-A0 learners. | moderate | Task complexity can trade off accuracy and fluency in novice learners. |
| `PRN-058` | After an independent speaking error, prefer a short opportunity for self-repair when plausible; after correction/reveal, label the next repetition as supported relearning. | moderate | Oral feedback helps learning; prompts can beneficially require modified output. |
| `PRN-059` | Do not equate faster or longer speech with global speaking mastery. | high | Fluency measures are task-conditioned and do not alone establish communicative capability. |
| `PRN-060` | Do not use opaque ASR or pronunciation scores as speaking mastery gates before RQ-007/RQ-008 establish validity for the intended population. | high | Automated scoring validity is an unresolved dependency. |
| `PRN-061` | Calibrate speaking progression against delayed first-seen parallel production per learner minute and burden. | moderate | Immediate smoothness on trained material can overstate durable independent production. |
| `PRN-062` | Optimize pronunciation for intelligibility first and comprehensibility second; never use accentedness or native-likeness alone as a failure criterion. | high | Accent strength and actual understanding are distinct outcomes. |
| `PRN-063` | Use Vietnamese population findings as diagnostic priors, then require learner-level evidence before allocating substantial pronunciation practice. | high | L1 findings vary by learner, task, region and listener. |
| `PRN-064` | Near A0, prioritize preservation of information-bearing word shape—especially risky codas and clusters—before fine accent polish when learner evidence confirms the problem. | high | Vietnamese syllable/coda evidence directly implicates word-shape loss. |
| `PRN-065` | Give extra priority to pronunciation deviations that erase lexical or grammatical information, while keeping feature-specific evidence boundaries explicit. | high | Morphology-bearing final sounds can carry language information, not just accent detail. |
| `PRN-066` | Use functional load as one ranking signal for segmental contrasts, not as a universal Vietnamese phoneme curriculum. | moderate | General L2 evidence supports prioritization but is not a Vietnamese whitelist. |
| `PRN-067` | Prioritize lexical-stress correction when the deviation damages vowel/syllable realization or word identification; do not correct every harmless prominence difference. | high | Stress-error severity depends strongly on lexical consequences. |
| `PRN-068` | Teach vowel contrasts diagnostically around lexical information and actual learner/listener confusion rather than requiring one native-accent vowel inventory. | moderate | Vietnamese vowel issues are real but contrast- and listener-dependent. |
| `PRN-069` | Use sentence focus, timing and intonation where they reduce listener effort or carry communicative intent; do not impose native-like rhythm as an A0 accent badge. | moderate | Prosody matters for comprehensibility but native mimicry is not the target. |
| `PRN-070` | Embed pronunciation work inside meaningful listening/speaking tasks and require model-hidden changed-context production before claiming independent pronunciation transfer. | high | Controlled imitation alone is supported practice, not transfer. |
| `PRN-071` | Record target-feature realization, listener intelligibility, comprehensibility, support and scoring provenance separately rather than collapsing them into one pronunciation score. | high | These measurements answer different questions. |
| `PRN-072` | Sample pronunciation outcomes across more than one relevant listener background before treating a target as broadly communication-critical. | moderate | Listener background changes judgments and understanding. |
| `PRN-073` | If speech is reliably understood, do not force correction solely to erase a Vietnamese accent; optional accent refinement must remain learner-controlled. | high | Accent is not communication failure. |
| `PRN-074` | Do not use ASR/vendor pronunciation scores as mastery gates until RQ-008 validates them against intended human listener outcomes for this population and task. | high | Automated-scoring validity remains unresolved. |
| `PRN-075` | Calibrate pronunciation target ordering by delayed first-seen listener understanding gained per learner minute, not by immediate imitation accuracy alone. | moderate | Exact Nếp priorities and dose remain product hypotheses. |
