# RQ-001 — Independent lexical evidence near A0

## Decision summary

Nếp should **not** represent lexical knowledge as a single `known = true/false` fact.

For product decisions, a word or chunk should have an **evidence profile** made from observed attempts. The profile can become progressively stronger from recognition to recall, controlled changed-context use, and delayed re-demonstration. The exact thresholds remain a Nếp experiment, not a literature-derived constant.

This is an operational measurement model. It must **not** be described as proof that vocabulary consists of four independent psychological dimensions. Recent construct research found that several common word-knowledge measures can load on one underlying vocabulary-knowledge factor (`SRC-0009`).

## What the literature supports

### 1. Lexical knowledge is not adequately represented by a binary flag

Vocabulary frameworks distinguish knowledge of form, meaning, and use, with receptive and productive manifestations. Empirical work also shows that the same target can produce different results under different elicitation formats (`SRC-0002`, `SRC-0008`, `SRC-0009`, `SRC-0014`).

**Product consequence:** Nếp stores attempts and derived evidence states rather than one permanent mastery boolean.

### 2. Recognition is weaker evidence than recall

Laufer & Goldstein found a stable difficulty hierarchy in which recognition tasks were easier than recall tasks (`SRC-0008`). A 2025 analysis of 314 EFL learners also found recognition preceding recall across several word-knowledge components (`SRC-0010`). That 2025 paper shares the dataset used in related construct work, so it is not counted as an independent replication of `SRC-0009`.

**Product consequence:** multiple-choice success can establish recognition evidence only. It cannot silently upgrade an item to independent recall or productive availability.

### 3. Receptive availability does not imply productive availability

Receptive vocabulary size can exceed productive vocabulary size, particularly under fuller-knowledge scoring (`SRC-0016`). Collocation research likewise reports a stronger measurement base for receptive than productive knowledge (`SRC-0015`).

**Product consequence:** understanding a target when heard/read and producing it from meaning/context are separate observations even if they may reflect a shared underlying lexical construct.

### 4. The test determines the claim

Meta-analytic and assessment research shows that observed learning changes with recognition vs recall format, scoring, cueing, prior-knowledge control, and retention interval (`SRC-0008`, `SRC-0011`, `SRC-0012`, `SRC-0015`, `SRC-0016`).

**Product consequence:** every attempt must record the construct and support level. A first-letter cue, visible translation, word bank, or answer choices change what the attempt can support.

### 5. Immediate success is not retention

A meta-analysis of intentional word-focused activities found large immediate gains that fell substantially on delayed meaning- and form-recall tests (`SRC-0012`). Incidental-learning meta-analysis also found meaningful differences across immediate and delayed tests (`SRC-0011`).

**Product consequence:** `retained` requires a later independent performance. Completing a lesson or succeeding immediately cannot set retained/mastered state.

### 6. Translation pairs alone are too narrow

Adult laboratory research argues for high-quality orthographic, phonological, and meaning representations and strong connections among them. Repetition alone is weak when it mostly strengthens L1→L2 form links; retrieval, spacing, and semantic elaboration can strengthen learning (`SRC-0013`). Word-card research also systematically undermeasures productive knowledge and knowledge of use (`SRC-0014`).

**Product consequence:** for a listening-first product, written translation recall cannot stand in for recognizing the spoken form. Nếp should deliberately collect aural evidence as well as written/form-meaning evidence.

### 7. Chunks need phrase-level evidence and explicit cue tracking

A recent systematic review of 153 collocation studies found many receptive instruments but far fewer validated productive measures, and highlighted how task formats and cues change the inference (`SRC-0015`).

**Product consequence:** chunks such as `I'd like` or `come back` are lexical targets in their own right. If Nếp reveals part of the phrase or its first letters, the resulting attempt is scaffolded evidence, not independent phrase production.

## Tension in the literature: dimensions vs one construct

Two statements can both be true:

1. different tasks reveal meaningfully different performances and should not be conflated in product measurement; and
2. those performances may be strongly interrelated indicators of one underlying lexical-knowledge construct.

`SRC-0009` provides evidence for the second claim. Therefore Nếp should avoid language such as "four scientifically independent vocabulary dimensions." `understood`, `recalled`, `transferred`, and `retained` are **product evidence categories tied to different tasks and times**, not a claim about four separate mental systems.

## Proposed Nếp lexical evidence ladder

This is the current product synthesis, not a validated universal scale.

| State | Minimum evidence | What it does **not** prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `introduced` | learner has encountered the target | any knowledge |
| `recognized` | correct unsupported meaning/form recognition in the tested modality | recall or use |
| `retrievable` | correct recall without the answer visible; support level recorded | changed-context use or retention |
| `usable` | correct controlled use/comprehension in a changed parallel context | spontaneous general use or long-term retention |
| `retained` | relevant independent evidence succeeds after a delay | permanent mastery |

The states should be **derived from attempt history**, not stored as irreversible truth. Forgetting can lower confidence and trigger new evidence collection.

## Modality rules near A0

Nếp should separately know what kind of evidence exists:

- `aural_meaning_recognition`: learner hears the target and identifies/derives its meaning;
- `written_meaning_recognition`: learner reads the target and identifies/derives its meaning;
- `meaning_recall`: learner retrieves meaning from L2 form without seeing the answer;
- `form_recall`: learner retrieves L2 form from meaning/context without the full form visible;
- `controlled_use`: learner selects/builds/says the target appropriately in a changed context;
- `delayed_*`: the same construct re-demonstrated after an interval.

Nếp does **not** need every cell to be strong before the curriculum can move forward. Initial A0 progression should emphasize useful form-meaning connections, especially spoken form for a listening-first experience, while gradually deepening collocations, multiple meanings, morphology, and constraints on use (`SRC-0009`, `SRC-0010`).

## What counts as "independent" in the product

For RQ-001, an attempt is independent only relative to the claim being made.

- visible answer/translation → not independent retrieval;
- multiple choice → can support recognition, not recall;
- first-letter or partial-phrase cue → scaffolded form recall, not uncued production;
- repeating immediately after a model → imitation, not independent recall;
- same-context retry → useful correction evidence, not transfer;
- delayed parallel task with answer hidden → stronger retention evidence.

Every attempt should therefore record `support_level`, `input_mode`, `response_mode`, and whether the context is same/parallel/changed.

## Do not conclude

- Recognition is useless. It is valid evidence for a recognition claim.
- Every target must reach productive free use before the learner can continue.
- The 75%/80% thresholds used in published scaling analyses are Nếp mastery thresholds.
- One delayed interval (for example 24 hours or 7 days) is universally optimal.
- A single composite vocabulary score can replace task-level evidence.
- The literature has validated Nếp's exact five-state ladder; the ladder is a product synthesis requiring calibration.

## Product decisions unlocked by RQ-001

1. Build `FEAT-VOC-001 Vocabulary evidence model`.
2. Remove/avoid irreversible `known` or `mastered` booleans as the sole lexical state.
3. Require every lexical task type to declare the inference it supports.
4. Store support/cue level with each attempt.
5. Keep immediate and delayed evidence separate.
6. Treat words and high-value chunks as first-class lexical targets.
7. Let the curriculum use graded evidence rather than waiting for fictitious perfect mastery.

## Still unresolved

- Which evidence combination should unlock the next A0 item?
- What delay(s) are feasible and predictive for `retained` in Nếp users?
- How much partial credit should speech/spelling errors receive when meaning is communicatively clear?
- What changed-context task best measures controlled lexical transfer without requiring too much grammar at near A0?
- How should confidence decay after contradictory later attempts?

These move into `EXP-001` and later RQ-003/RQ-005 work.
