# RQ-010 source digest

Question: **When should English captions, Vietnamese support, transcript and replay appear, and when should they be removed?**

## Evidence picture

The literature strongly supports captions as useful scaffolds for comprehension and lexical learning, but it does **not** support one universal display policy. Effects change with learner proficiency, caption reliance, task goal, material complexity and measurement method.

### High-value findings

- Meta-analytic work supports captioned viewing for listening comprehension and vocabulary outcomes, while test type and study design matter (`SRC-0098`, `SRC-0110`).
- Full captions can outperform keyword captions for global comprehension in some studies; keyword/partial captions are not consistently superior simply because they show less text (`SRC-0099`, `SRC-0104`).
- Eye-tracking and reliance studies show lower-proficiency learners often allocate substantial attention to captions and can rely on text rather than audio (`SRC-0101`, `SRC-0102`, `SRC-0103`, `SRC-0108`).
- Caption reliance varies by individual, not only by CEFR/proficiency; learners with different reliance profiles can benefit from different caption modes (`SRC-0103`, `SRC-0104`).
- L1 subtitles can improve content comprehension, especially when content is difficult, but they create a direct semantic route that cannot be interpreted as independent L2 listening (`SRC-0094`, `SRC-0109`).
- Repeated viewing generally gives learners additional opportunities to process meaning/form; however, caption order findings are mixed and do not justify a universal "always audio first" or "always captions first" scientific rule (`SRC-0100`, `SRC-0102`).
- Progressive caption reduction has encouraging learner-perception evidence, but current direct causal evidence is too weak to prescribe a fixed full → partial → keyword → none sequence (`SRC-0105`).
- Caption availability can continue to improve *content comprehension* even at relatively high proficiency; therefore a caption-off threshold for content viewing is not the same as a caption-off rule for listening training (`SRC-0107`).
- Learner preferences and caption use can change over time; higher-proficiency learners may naturally move toward uncaptioned viewing, while elementary learners can continue to need L1 support for leisure comprehension (`SRC-0106`).

## Critical gap

There is little direct evidence establishing the optimal **adaptive sequence** of replay, L2 caption, L1 micro-gloss, full transcript and full L1 translation for Vietnamese near-A0 learners. That exact policy remains a product experiment.

## Product interpretation

Nếp should model support by the **information it reveals**, not as a single scalar ladder:

```text
support action         reveals form?   reveals meaning?   preserves audio-first evidence?
replay audio/video         no               no                    yes*
L2 keyword cue             partial          little                no for cued target
L2 full caption            yes              indirect              no for form/segmentation
Vietnamese micro-gloss     little           partial               no for glossed meaning
L2 transcript              full             indirect              no for form/segmentation
L1 full subtitle           little           full                  no for message meaning
full translation           little           full                  no for message meaning
```

`*` Replay preserves the absence of answer-bearing text, but it is a repeated exposure. It is not fresh first-seen evidence.

This support-provenance model should govern both learning behavior and how evidence is interpreted.
