# RQ-022 source digest — foundational SLA mechanisms and competing instructional theories

**RQ:** Which learning mechanisms and instructional theories have the strongest converging evidence for adult instructed L2 development, which predictions conflict, and what does each imply for Vietnamese near-A0 self-study?

**Status:** initial meta-foundation evidence digest  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Registry:** `data/sources-rq022.json`, `data/claims-rq022.json`

## 1. Why this RQ is different from RQ-001–RQ-021

Earlier cycles mostly asked local questions:

- how to review;
- how to scaffold listening;
- how to introduce speaking;
- when captions help;
- what counts as transfer;
- how AI should be gated.

RQ-022 asks whether the **foundational picture tying these together is defensible**.

The purpose is not to pick a fashionable SLA theory. It is to compare what different theories/evidence families explain well, where their instructional predictions overlap or conflict, and what the evidence does **not** justify.

A theory is not scored as a product feature. The relevant question is:

```text
what mechanism does it predict?
→ what evidence tests that mechanism?
→ what outcome was measured?
→ what boundary conditions appear?
→ what instructional condition survives comparison?
```

## 3. Broad instruction and form-focused evidence

### SRC-0235 — Norris & Ortega (2000)

Key contribution:

- synthesized 49 unique-sample experimental/quasi-experimental studies;
- focused L2 instruction produced large target-oriented gains;
- explicit treatments were stronger than implicit treatments on average;
- Focus on Form and Focus on Forms both produced large effects;
- effects appeared durable in available posttests;
- crucially, outcome-measure type affected effect size;
- authors explicitly warned that poor construct operationalization and replication limited generalizability.

What this source **does support**:

```text
instruction can accelerate targeted L2 learning
```

What it **does not support**:

```text
explicit grammar explanation
→ automatically becomes spontaneous communicative competence
```

The source itself makes outcome measurement part of the interpretation problem.

### SRC-0236 — Kang, Sok & Han (2019)

54 studies / 5,051 learners across 35 years of ISLA form-focused research.

Findings relevant here:

- large overall instructional effect;
- only a minor difference between explicit and implicit instruction in this later corpus;
- outcome measure, starting proficiency, setting and instructional intensity were significant moderators.

Why it matters:

Earlier syntheses made the explicit advantage look simple. The later evidence shows a more conditional picture.

### SRC-0237 — Spada & Tomita (2010)

41 studies of simple/complex grammatical features.

- explicit instruction had larger effects than implicit instruction for both feature types;
- benefits appeared on controlled knowledge and measures intended to capture more spontaneous use.

Boundary:

“spontaneous” measures are still measurement instruments, not unrestricted real-world interaction. RQ-024 must revisit how much inference such tests warrant.

### SRC-0238 — Goo et al. (2015)

Updated Norris & Ortega's explicit/implicit question.

Important for the meta-foundation because it reinforces that:

- treatment definitions matter;
- explicit/implicit knowledge cannot be inferred simply from an intervention label;
- the field's tests are a major source of apparent treatment differences.

## 4. Comprehension/input processing versus production

### SRC-0239 — Shintani, Li & Ellis (2013)

Meta-analysis directly comparing comprehension-based instruction (CBI) and production-based instruction (PBI).

35 projects in 30 studies.

Main pattern:

```text
receptive outcome, short term
→ CBI advantage

receptive outcome, delayed
→ difference diminishes

productive outcome, short term
→ similar

productive outcome, delayed
→ PBI advantage
```

The initial CBI advantage was largely attributable to Processing Instruction studies.

This is central to RQ-022 because it rejects two universal stories at once:

```text
“input is enough”
```

and

```text
“production practice is always superior”
```

The outcome targeted matters.

### SRC-0240 — Shintani (2015)

42 experiments / 33 studies of Processing Instruction (PI) and production-based instruction.

- PI stronger for receptive knowledge;
- production practice as effective for productive knowledge overall;
- when explicit information was equalized, production-based instruction was superior for productive knowledge;
- age, long-term effects, strategy training and target processing principle moderated outcomes.

Implication for theory comparison:

Processing Instruction has strong evidence for **changing how learners process form–meaning cues in input**. That is a mechanism-specific success, not proof it should become the entire curriculum.

## 5. Interaction, output and feedback

### SRC-0241 — Mackey & Goo (2007)

Interaction research synthesis/meta-analysis supports a facilitative role for interaction in L2 learning.

Interaction is theoretically important because one episode can combine:

```text
input
→ comprehension problem
→ negotiation / clarification
→ feedback
→ attention
→ modified output / repair
```

This means “interaction works” does not identify one single causal ingredient.

For Nếp research, interaction should be decomposed by task and evidence rather than treated as generic chat time.

### SRC-0242 — Lyster & Saito (2010)

Classroom oral corrective-feedback meta-analysis.

- oral corrective feedback produced significant durable effects;
- prompts showed advantages over recasts in the analyzed corpus;
- outcome measure, learner age and treatment characteristics moderated results.

This is enough to establish feedback as a serious learning mechanism, but **not enough to define Nếp's universal feedback algorithm**.

That belongs to RQ-025.

## 6. Practice, proceduralization and automaticity

### SRC-0243 — Suzuki, Maie & Hui (2025)

Research timeline synthesizing four decades of automatization research.

Key distinction:

```text
knowing language information
≠
using language efficiently in real time
```

Automatization research supports systematic practice as part of moving from effortful processing toward fast/stable/efficient performance.

But good practice in this literature is broader than audiolingual drill. It can be:

- deliberate;
- systematic;
- contextualized;
- feedback-informed;
- transfer-appropriate;
- varied as the skill stabilizes.

Also important:

```text
faster performance
≠ automatically true automaticity
```

because speed-up can occur without broader stability/restructuring.

### SRC-0244 — Maie & Godfroid (2025)

A particularly important source because Skill Acquisition Theory's three-stage account had often been assumed rather than directly tested.

Design:

- 73 participants;
- explicit-deductive learning of a miniature language;
- repeated comprehension practice;
- hidden Markov models compared rival one-, two- and three-stage accounts.

Finding:

- data supported a three-stage trajectory;
- declarative learning ability was especially important initially;
- procedural-learning measures contributed more to later performance patterns.

Why this does **not** settle Nếp's teaching order:

- miniature language;
- explicit learning condition;
- intensive controlled comprehension practice;
- no proof that natural English lexical, pronunciation, pragmatic and interaction development must all follow the exact same path.

So the correct status is:

```text
stronger support for SAT than the repo previously acknowledged
+
meaningful generalization limits
```

## 7. Usage-based / construction learning

### SRC-0245 and SRC-0246 — Ellis & Wulff / Wulff & Ellis

Core proposal:

```text
language knowledge
≈ network of form ↔ meaning/function constructions
```

Learning is sensitive to:

- token/type frequency;
- form salience;
- form–function contingency;
- prototypicality;
- exemplar diversity;
- recency;
- L1-tuned learned attention/blocking.

Important correction to a simplistic “input frequency” interpretation:

> A frequent form is not necessarily an easy form.

Articles, prepositions and low-salience morphology can remain difficult despite enormous exposure.

Usage-based researchers themselves therefore discuss form-focused/explicit support as a way to recruit adult conscious attention before richer experience can consolidate associations.

This is one of the strongest convergence points with Processing Instruction and form-focused instruction:

```text
meaningful exposure matters
but
adult attention may systematically miss useful cues
```

## 8. Attention, noticing and input enhancement

### SRC-0247 — Lee & Huang (2008)

16 primary studies / 20 samples on visual input enhancement.

Key result:

- enhanced text barely outperformed flooded but unenhanced text on grammar learning on average (`d ≈ 0.22` in the synthesis);
- a small negative effect appeared for meaning processing.

This source matters because it breaks a common product shortcut:

```text
bold / color / underline target form
→ learner notices
→ learner learns
```

That chain is not supported.

Perceptual salience can help attention, but a learning task may need the learner to actually resolve a **form–meaning problem** rather than merely see a highlighted form.

## 9. Intentional versus incidental vocabulary learning

This comparison is useful because it shows why RQ-022 should not choose one learning mechanism exclusively.

### SRC-0248 — Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara (2020)

100 effect sizes from 22 studies of intentional word-focused activities.

Average gains were high immediately on meaning/form recall, but dropped substantially on delayed tests.

What this supports:

- deliberate vocabulary work can be very efficient;
- different word-focused activities vary substantially;
- initial intentional learning does not guarantee durable knowledge.

It does not establish that decontextualized word learning alone builds language use.

### SRC-0249 — Webb, Uchihara & Yanagisawa (2023)

24 primary studies / 2,771 participants on incidental vocabulary from meaning-focused input.

Average proportion of target words picked up was modest:

- roughly 9–18% on immediate tests depending on measure/mode;
- roughly 6–17% on delayed tests.

Reading/listening/reading-while-listening supported incidental learning; viewing was lower in the analyzed uncaptained conditions.

This means:

```text
incidental learning is real
```

but also:

```text
one understandable encounter
≠ efficient acquisition of all unknown vocabulary
```

Especially for near-zero learners with limited language exposure, relying on incidental pickup alone would require very large exposure volumes.

### SRC-0250 — de Vos et al. (2018)

32 studies / 1,964 participants on incidental word learning from spoken input.

- strong overall effect relative to controls;
- adults outperformed children in the analyzed data;
- interactive tasks were more effective than noninteractive tasks.

This links incidental lexical learning back to interaction rather than supporting passive exposure as a complete method.

## 10. Sociocultural Theory and mediated development

### SRC-0251 — van Compernolle & Williams (2013)

Sociocultural Theory (SCT) differs from purely cognitive accounts in how it conceptualizes development and mediation.

Relevant instructional ideas include:

- culturally mediated psychological tools;
- human mediation;
- Zone of Proximal Development;
- concept-based instruction;
- dynamic assessment.

It is a mistake to translate SCT into the generic slogan “scaffolding is good.”

The deeper implication is that learner ability can be investigated through **how performance changes under contingent assistance**.

### SRC-0252 — Lantolf & Poehner (2011)

Dynamic Assessment intentionally integrates assessment and instruction.

This creates a useful distinction for Nếp research:

```text
independent performance
≠
responsiveness to mediation / learning potential
```

The existing research repo was already right to avoid calling supported performance independent mastery.

But RQ-022 reveals that the support response itself may still be pedagogically and diagnostically valuable.

RQ-024 must decide how to represent both without mixing them.

## 11. Contemporary field map

### SRC-0253 — Loewen (2025)

The third edition of *Introduction to Instructed Second Language Acquisition* confirms that contemporary ISLA cannot be reduced to one input/output/grammar theory.

Relevant domains include:

- type of instruction;
- input and interaction;
- output;
- feedback;
- grammar;
- vocabulary;
- pronunciation;
- individual differences;
- technology-mediated learning;
- pedagogical implementation.

This source is used primarily to ensure **field coverage**, not to assign efficacy by authority.

## 12. Direct contradictions / tensions surfaced by RQ-022

### 12.1 Explicit versus implicit

Evidence does **not** support:

```text
explicit is useless
```

Nor does it support:

```text
explicit rule teaching alone creates implicit fluent ability
```

Current synthesis:

- explicit instruction is usually efficient for targeted learning;
- outcome measures partly determine apparent advantage;
- longer exposure and more implicit-sensitive outcomes are needed to compare deeper knowledge development fairly;
- explicit knowledge can be useful without being identical to spontaneous performance.

### 12.2 Input versus output

False dichotomy:

```text
input OR output
```

Current evidence:

- input/form–meaning processing is crucial for comprehension;
- production practice has distinct value for productive outcomes;
- relative advantage depends on target construct and delay.

### 12.3 Rule representation versus exemplar/construction learning

Skill Acquisition Theory emphasizes declarative knowledge that can be proceduralized under some instructional conditions.

Usage-based theory emphasizes accumulated form–meaning/function exemplars and emerging abstractions.

These are not fully interchangeable theories.

But product-level convergence exists:

- useful exemplars matter;
- repeated meaningful use matters;
- abstract knowledge alone is insufficient;
- adult learners can benefit from explicit attention when input cues are hard to learn naturally.

RQ-022 does **not** resolve the representational debate by declaring one theory scientifically defeated.

### 12.4 Independent assessment versus mediated development

Existing Nếp evidence logic emphasizes independent performance for strong evidence.

SCT/DA argues that responsiveness under mediation reveals developing capability/learning potential.

These can coexist if the system records them as different constructs rather than forcing one number:

```text
independent current capability
+
responsiveness to contingent assistance
```

RQ-024 owns the validity problem.

## 13. Target-population directness

A major limitation across RQ-022:

There is **very little direct evidence for Vietnamese adults beginning near zero in an adaptive self-study web/mobile system**.

Evidence directness varies:

```text
mechanism evidence
    often strong

exact target-population evidence
    often weak/moderate

exact Nếp delivery policy
    usually untested
```

Therefore RQ-022 can constrain the method but cannot specify exact lesson dosage or order.

## 14. What RQ-022 now makes hard to defend

### Reject as universal claims

```text
1. Comprehensible input alone is the optimal complete method.
2. Grammar rules + drills alone are the optimal complete method.
3. Learners should delay all production until a fixed vocabulary/level threshold.
4. Speaking volume itself guarantees acquisition.
5. Highlighting/noticing a form means the learner acquired it.
6. Explicit knowledge is the same as spontaneous language ability.
7. Incidental exposure is efficient enough to replace deliberate vocabulary work for a near-zero learner.
8. Intentional vocabulary memorization makes contextual exposure unnecessary.
9. A single successful assisted performance is independent mastery.
10. One theory label can determine the whole curriculum.
```

## 15. Provisional convergence — still not Nếp Method

The evidence reviewed so far converges on a combination more like:

```text
meaningful / comprehensible input
        +
processing of form ↔ meaning/function
        +
targeted explicit help when it solves a real learning/processing problem
        +
retrieval / production when productive ability is the goal
        +
interaction + contingent feedback when interactive ability is the goal
        +
deliberate/systematic practice toward efficient performance
        +
rich exemplars and contextual variation
        +
large amounts of later meaning-focused exposure
        +
scaffolding whose effect is distinguished from independent performance
```

This is a **constraint set**, not a fixed lesson template.

## 16. What remains unresolved after RQ-022

RQ-022 does not establish:

- the first capability to teach;
- the highest-value English content;
- exact input:output ratio;
- exact amount of explicit explanation;
- exact delay before production;
- exact number/type of exemplars;
- exact feedback policy;
- assessment validity thresholds;
- adaptations by anxiety/aptitude/WTC/SRL;
- Vietnamese near-A0 dosage;
- whether one sequence dominates another in the Nếp product context.

Those questions are deliberately routed to RQ-023–RQ-028.

## 17. Digest conclusion

RQ-022 changes the foundation from:

```text
pick one theory / method
```

to:

```text
define the target capability
→ identify the learning/processing problem
→ use the instructional mechanism with evidence for that outcome
→ provide enough meaningful experience and practice
→ preserve construct-specific evidence
→ test generalization over time/context
```

The scientific problem now becomes **how to integrate complementary mechanisms without turning every lesson into an overloaded checklist**.

That integration cannot be finalized until target needs, content validity, assessment validity, feedback and learner moderators are researched in P12.
