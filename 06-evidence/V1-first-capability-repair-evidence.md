# V1 First-Capability Evidence — Conversational Repair

**Status:** focused curriculum-derivation evidence note  
**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Capability:** initiate repair after global non-understanding  
**Parent:** `03-curriculum/FIRST_VALIDATION_CAPABILITY_REPAIR.md`

---

## 1. Research question

For a Vietnamese near-A0 adult learning English, what can current evidence justify about teaching the first interaction-repair capability?

Subquestions:

1. What kinds of repair initiators occur in natural English/ELF interaction?
2. Should the learner memorize one phrase or develop a small repertoire?
3. Can repair be taught from the beginning?
4. What type of practice is needed for interactional use rather than phrase recall?
5. Are register/cultural choices simple enough to hard-code?

---

## 2. Natural interaction does not support a one-phrase model

### `SRC-0357` — Kendrick (2015), *Other-initiated repair in English*

Natural English conversation distinguishes:

```text
open repair
→ whole prior turn is treated as problematic

specific repair
→ a particular trouble source is located
```

Open forms include items such as `what?`, `huh?`, `pardon?`, `sorry?`, while specific formats include question words, partial/full repeats and candidate understandings.

Implication:

```text
repair capability
≠ exact recall of one sentence
```

### `SRC-0358` — Dingemanse et al. (2015)

Cross-linguistic natural-conversation data from 12 languages show a recurrent organization around:

- open request;
- restricted/specific request;
- restricted offer/candidate understanding.

More specific forms generally place more of the diagnostic/repair work on the recipient, while open forms leave more work to the trouble-source speaker.

Implication:

A curriculum can reasonably progress from a minimal/global repair action toward more specific repair practices as learner resources grow, but the exact English sequence remains a curriculum hypothesis.

---

## 3. English as a lingua franca evidence strengthens the repertoire view

### `SRC-0361` — Thongphut & Kaur (2025; online 2024)

Fifteen hours of naturally occurring hospitality/tourism English-as-a-lingua-franca service encounters were analyzed.

Reported repair-initiator distribution included substantial use of both:

- open-class repair initiators;
- candidate understandings.

Within the open-class category, formulaic `pardon?/pardon me/sorry?` forms were frequent in this service corpus, alongside interjections and other forms.

Repair solutions after an open initiator could combine:

```text
repetition
+ lexical replacement
+ rephrasing
+ explication
```

Implications:

1. `Sorry?` is empirically attested in multilingual service interaction and is a legitimate candidate resource.
2. It must not be treated as the only correct repair action.
3. The partner's repair response is itself part of the interactional sequence; the learning task should continue through the response rather than ending immediately after the learner says a phrase.
4. Domain frequency does not prove a universal first-form ranking.

---

## 4. Repair can be introduced to absolute beginners, but comparative efficacy is not established

### `SRC-0359` — Kaneyasu (2026; online 2025)

The study analyzed 17 hours of first-semester beginning Japanese classes, with many absolute beginners.

The pedagogy introduced repair early and emphasized:

1. awareness of what repair does in interaction;
2. repeated opportunities for real-time use;
3. semi-spontaneous/unrehearsed interaction rather than only drills;
4. timely feedback when learners failed to signal trouble;
5. gradual expansion from simpler/open repair to more specific practices.

The paper argues that explicit knowledge of repair forms alone does not readily become procedural interactional ability; learners need contextual, real-time practice.

Important limitation:

```text
classroom design evidence
≠ controlled proof that this intervention is superior
```

The study is Japanese-as-L2, university classroom data and does not directly establish English learning efficacy for Vietnamese near-A0 self-study.

Implication for Nếp:

The safe design hypothesis is not:

```text
teach phrase
→ quiz phrase
→ capability
```

but:

```text
notice repair function
→ practice a small resource
→ encounter genuine/semi-genuine breakdown
→ choose repair in real time
→ receive contingent partner response
→ feedback if needed
→ repeat under changed conditions
```

## 5. Vietnamese L1 repair evidence adds a cultural-pragmatic warning

### `SRC-0360` — Sidnell & Vũ (2024)

Natural Vietnamese conversation showed repair-format selection associated with locally relevant seniority relations in the sampled Hanoi conversations.

Seniors and juniors did not always distribute the interactional work of repair in the same way.

This matters because a Vietnamese learner may arrive with L1 interactional expectations about:

- who should make the effort to clarify;
- how explicitly to display non-understanding;
- how much of the prior turn to reconstruct before requesting repair.

But the correct inference is:

```text
Vietnamese L1 pattern
→ cultural/pragmatic prior to probe

NOT

Vietnamese learner
→ fixed English behavior
```

English register/politeness instruction must therefore remain context-sensitive and directly validated.

## 6. `Sorry?` is a candidate, not the curriculum

Current evidence supports this bounded statement:

> `Sorry?` is a naturally attested open repair initiator in English and multilingual workplace/service interaction and is a plausible short-form resource for a near-A0 learner.

Current evidence does **not** support:

> `Sorry?` is universally the politest, most frequent, safest, or optimal first repair form for every context.

Natural interaction also includes:

```text
What?
Huh?
Pardon?
partial repetition
X?
X what?
candidate understanding
explicit repetition request
```

These forms differ in specificity, social meaning, context and linguistic load.

Therefore the first slice should teach **repair as a function plus a small repertoire**, with one short form receiving initial production focus for tractability.

---

## 7. Revised first-slice hypothesis

### Phase 1 — Global/open repair

Learner understands the function and can orally deploy one short open repair initiator when the whole prior turn is not available.

Candidate initial production form:

```text
Sorry?
```

but functional alternatives are not automatically wrong.

### Phase 2 — Explicit repetition request

Add a longer explicit resource such as:

```text
Could you say that again?
```

as a formulaic chunk once the learner can handle the added phonological/working-memory load.

### Phase 3 — Specific repair

When the learner heard part of the turn, introduce practices such as:

```text
partial repeat + rising intonation
specific question word
candidate understanding
```

This progression is **evidence-informed but not yet target-population validated**.

---

## 8. Task-design consequences

A valid learning task must eventually create this decision:

```text
I understood enough
→ respond normally

OR

I did not understand
→ initiate repair
```

If every screen says:

> “Say: Could you say that again?”

then the task measures prompted phrase production, not repair selection.

A stronger validation task has:

- a prior spoken turn;
- uncertainty about whether the learner understood;
- more than one legitimate next action;
- no answer-bearing repair text visible;
- oral response;
- a partner response contingent on the learner's action;
- preservation of support/replay exposure;
- changed-context and delayed re-probes.

---

## 9. Scoring consequence

Strong functional evidence is not:

```text
ASR transcript == target string
```

It is closer to:

```text
learner signals repair orally
→ partner/listener recognizes repair intent
→ partner repeats/rephrases/clarifies
→ interaction can continue
```

Transcript/form accuracy can remain a secondary diagnostic dimension.

## 10. Claims added

- `CLM-INT-018` — beginner repair teaching design evidence: awareness + real-time semi-spontaneous use + feedback + progressive repertoire; comparative efficacy unresolved.
- `CLM-INT-019` — ELF natural interaction supports flexible repair sequence/repertoire rather than one exact phrase.
- `CLM-INT-020` — Vietnamese L1 repair behavior can be a pragmatic prior, not an English learner diagnosis.

---

## 11. Decision

Keep the first capability:

> **Initiate repair after global non-understanding.**

But revise its teaching interpretation from:

```text
learn one phrase for “say it again”
```

into:

```text
understand repair as an interactional action
→ establish one low-load spoken resource
→ practice choosing it at genuine breakdown moments
→ preserve functional alternatives
→ expand toward more specific repair as resources grow
```

This is the strongest design currently justified by the evidence reviewed here.
