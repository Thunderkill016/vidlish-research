# EXP-023 — Direct target-needs study for Nếp

**Status:** planned research study  
**Depends on:** RQ-023  
**Purpose:** replace owner/AI intuition with primary evidence about what intended Nếp learners actually need to do in English.

## 1. Primary question

Which English target tasks should define the portable beginner core for Vietnamese-speaking adults near Pre-A1/A1, and which needs are domain-specific enough to branch?

## 2. Population

Recruit intended learner types rather than one institutional class.

Minimum sampling dimensions:

- near-A0 / A1 evidence level;
- employed / job-seeking / student / other adult status;
- broad occupational sectors where feasible;
- age bands within adult scope;
- urban / non-urban where feasible;
- immediate English-use expectation versus aspirational learning;
- available study time.

Do not treat the study as nationally representative unless the sample design actually supports that claim.

## 3. Phase A — learner interviews / task elicitation

Use semi-structured interviews.

Avoid prompts such as:

```text
Do you want to improve speaking?
```

Prefer:

```text
Think of the last time English stopped you from doing something useful.
What were you trying to do?
Who/what were you interacting with?
Was it spoken, written or both?
What happened?
```

and:

```text
In the next 6–24 months, in what situations are you most likely to need English?
What would success look like there?
```

Output:
- raw target-task candidates;
- context;
- participants;
- channel;
- desired outcome;
- consequence of failure.

## 4. Phase B — insider triangulation

For high-frequency domains, recruit people who can already perform the target tasks:

- recent successful workers;
- employers/supervisors where relevant;
- domain instructors;
- experienced students/graduates for study tasks.

Ask insiders to:
- generate missing target tasks;
- correct unrealistic learner assumptions;
- describe success criteria;
- identify common failure modes;
- estimate need for training.

## 5. Phase C — task-rating survey

Create a task inventory from phases A/B.

For each task collect:

```text
frequency
importance / consequence
current difficulty
urgency
need for training
```

Also collect domain relevance:

```text
personal
public
educational
professional
```

Do not collapse these dimensions into one score before inspecting their distributions.

## 6. Phase D — authentic target-discourse sampling

For the highest-priority candidate tasks, obtain authentic or securely de-identified examples where feasible:

- short conversations/calls;
- messages/chat;
- email;
- forms;
- interface text;
- instructions;
- service exchanges;
- recruitment materials.

Analyze:
- recurring functions;
- information structure;
- formulaic sequences;
- vocabulary/constructions;
- pragmatic moves;
- repair behavior;
- accent/interlocutor variability;
- channel constraints.

This phase feeds RQ-027.

## 7. Candidate portable-core hypothesis

Test whether these families recur strongly enough across domains to justify a core:

1. exchange essential identifying/context information;
2. understand and answer a short question/request;
3. ask for clarification/repetition/confirmation;
4. exchange number/time/date/price/place/contact details;
5. make and respond to a simple request;
6. read a short practical message/instruction/interface text;
7. write a short request/confirmation/update;
8. open/maintain/close a short interaction;
9. recover from a misunderstanding or unknown item.

These are hypotheses, not preselected curriculum content.

## 8. Candidate prioritization model

Do not pre-commit to one formula.

A candidate analysis may inspect:

```text
priority = f(
  cross-domain prevalence,
  frequency,
  consequence/importance,
  urgency,
  current difficulty,
  training need,
  prerequisite value,
  transfer/portability
)
```

Weights are research decisions and should be sensitivity-tested.

## 9. Outputs

The study should produce:

- target-task inventory;
- domain map;
- ratings with uncertainty;
- candidate portable core;
- candidate domain branches;
- target-discourse sample set/metadata;
- disagreement report across learners and insiders;
- tasks explicitly excluded or deferred;
- RQ-027 input package.

## 10. Decision rule

Do not freeze a capability into the portable core only because:

- it is common in textbooks;
- CEFR mentions it;
- the owner likes it;
- learners say the broad skill sounds important;
- one occupation uses it frequently.

A portable-core capability needs converging evidence of usefulness across a meaningful portion of the intended learner population or strong prerequisite/transfer value.

## 11. Falsification

The candidate core must change if direct data show that:

- assumed common tasks are rare or low-value;
- important tasks were omitted;
- domain branching should happen earlier;
- literacy/digital tasks matter more than expected;
- productive tasks are less urgent than receptive survival tasks for a subgroup;
- learner goals cluster into distinct tracks rather than one portable sequence.
