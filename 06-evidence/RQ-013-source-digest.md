# RQ-013 source digest

## Research question

How should near-A0 learners progress from supported sentence-level reading to independent connected-text reading without confusing word recognition, comprehension, fluency and support-assisted success?

## Reading is multi-component

### SRC-0134 — Jeon & Yamashita (2014)
Large meta-analysis of correlates of passage-level L2 reading comprehension. Vocabulary, grammar, decoding and listening comprehension all show substantial relationships with reading comprehension. This argues against reducing reading to one quiz score or one subskill.

### SRC-0135 — Hamada et al. (2024)
Secondary meta-analysis / meta-analytic structural equation modeling supporting a Simple-View-like account in L2 reading: decoding and language-comprehension skills both contribute to reading comprehension, with language comprehension contributing more strongly in the tested model. Useful for separating written-word access from message understanding.

### SRC-0143 — Grabe & Yamashita
State-of-the-field chapter on L2 reading fluency. Fluency includes more than raw speed; automaticity, accuracy, rate and prosodic/text-level processing are connected to comprehension. Passage-level fluency predicts comprehension, but speed alone is not reading mastery.

## Vocabulary load and text difficulty

### SRC-0136 — Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe (2011)
Lexical coverage and reading comprehension are positively related. The amount of coverage required depends on the comprehension criterion and text; figures around 95–98% are useful research anchors, not universal product cutoffs.

`SRC-0088` (Webb, 2021) already in the repository provides the critical-review boundary: lexical-coverage thresholds are probabilistic and vary across learners, texts and outcome definitions.

### SRC-0140 — Yang, Chu & Tseng (2021)
Direct extensive-reading difficulty study. In this population, reading material one level below learners' estimated level produced stronger comprehension than material one level above. Useful evidence that easy text is not pedagogically inferior simply because it is easier; scope is limited to the studied learners/materials.

## Sustained / extensive reading

### SRC-0132 — Nakanishi (2015)
Meta-analysis of 34 extensive-reading studies. Extensive reading improved reading proficiency relative to comparison conditions, including effects across reading speed, comprehension and related language outcomes. Study characteristics and program implementation vary substantially.

### SRC-0133 — Sangers et al. (2025)
Updated meta-analysis across second/foreign-language extensive reading. Effects were positive across multiple language domains, generally small to medium. Text selection attuned to reading level and some form of accountability were associated with stronger effects. Long-term follow-up evidence remains sparse.

## Reading strategies

### SRC-0137 — Yapp, de Graaff & van den Bergh (2021)
Meta-analysis of 46 L2 reading-strategy studies found a positive overall effect on comprehension, with heterogeneity across strategies and pedagogical approaches. Only a small minority of included studies used delayed posttests, limiting retention claims.

### SRC-0138 — Ueno, Ikeda, Takeuchi & Teng (2025)
Updated meta-analysis targeting English L2 reading-strategy instruction. Strategy instruction improved both reported strategy use and reading comprehension on average, while effects varied by learner and context. Useful support for strategy training, not for a strategy-heavy near-A0 curriculum.

## Audio-assisted reading

### SRC-0139 — Clinton-Lisell (2023)
Systematic review/meta-analysis of reading-while-listening versus reading-only comprehension. Overall benefit was trivial; when reading-only was self-paced there was no reliable advantage for reading-while-listening. Some subgroups may benefit, but L2-specific generalization is limited.

This strongly supports treating audio as an optional scaffold, not as proof of independent reading.

## Fluency and Vietnamese learners

### SRC-0141 — Gorsuch & Taguchi (2008)
Eleven-week repeated-reading study with university-level Vietnamese EFL learners. The treatment group improved reading fluency and comprehension relative to comparison learners. Population relevance is valuable, but this does not establish the optimal repetition dose or near-A0 policy.

### SRC-0142 — Pham, Baker & Nguyen (2024)
Vietnamese undergraduate EFL experiment comparing unassisted and audio-assisted repeated reading. Both increased reading rate; audio-assisted repeated reading produced larger rate gains in the study. Outcome emphasis was reading rate, so it cannot establish independent comprehension mastery or a universal audio policy.

## Existing evidence reused

- `SRC-0025`: meta-analysis of glossing in L2 reading.
- `SRC-0017`: meta-analysis comparing L1 and L2 glosses.
- `SRC-0088`: critical review of lexical coverage.
- `SRC-0024`: multimedia gloss meta-analysis.

These support bounded lookup/gloss scaffolds, while provenance rules from `RQ-002` remain necessary.

## Main evidence gap

The literature does not establish one optimal progression for Vietnamese literate adults near A0 from sentence reading to microtexts to sustained graded reading. It also does not provide universal cutoffs for lexical coverage, reading rate, text length, audio fading or strategy instruction.

Those product parameters belong to `EXP-013`.
