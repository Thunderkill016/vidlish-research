# RQ-027 — Source digest: corpus-informed beginner content selection

**Status:** initial meta-foundation evidence digest  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Sources:** `SRC-0321`–`SRC-0342`  
**Claims:** `CLM-CONT-001`–`CLM-CONT-026`

## Research question

> Which words, chunks, constructions, functions and pragmatic routines deserve scarce beginner learning time when target needs are combined with real-language frequency, range, dispersion, coverage, formulaicity, learnability and prerequisite value?

The purpose is not to find one master word list. It is to establish what corpus evidence can and cannot justify when selecting content for Vietnamese adults beginning near zero.

## 1. Word lists are evidence, not curricula

`SRC-0321` reviews applications of word lists in L2 learning/teaching. Lists can support lexical profiling, identify likely unknown items and estimate whether materials recycle useful vocabulary. Their usefulness depends on the list's quality and purpose.

`SRC-0322` shows that a general core can be derived from large corpora, while `SRC-0327` gives a methodology for building a practical beginner list. Neither establishes a teaching sequence.

`SRC-0328` is especially relevant to Nếp: different defensible high-frequency lists were evaluated with teachers and Vietnamese EFL learners, and the lists did not behave identically. This is direct evidence against treating one published ranking as ground truth.

## 2. Frequency needs range, dispersion and corpus fit

`SRC-0323` demonstrates why token frequency alone can be misleading: an item may be frequent because it is concentrated in a narrow subset of texts. Range/dispersion describe whether use is broadly distributed.

`SRC-0324` treats corpus representativeness and replication as empirical questions. A specialized list generalizes only to the extent that the source corpus represents the target discourse.

`SRC-0342` directly examines whether corpus frequency alone is a good yardstick for pedagogical word selection and reinforces the need to consider distribution and pedagogical thresholds.

Product implication at the research level:

```text
frequency
+
range / dispersion
+
representative target corpus
```

is stronger evidence than raw rank alone.

## 3. The lexical counting unit changes the answer

`SRC-0325` and `SRC-0326` show that word-family, lemma and flemma choices materially affect vocabulary-size estimates and pedagogical interpretation.

For near-A0 learners, assuming that knowing one base form means all derivational family members are transparent risks overstating knowledge.

Therefore Nếp must not store a claim such as:

```text
learner knows word family X
```

unless the exact lexical unit and inference are defined.

## 4. Coverage is useful but not a universal threshold

`SRC-0329`, `SRC-0330` and `SRC-0332` support a strong relation between known-word percentage and reading comprehension. Around 98% is often a useful region for relatively independent reading, but `SRC-0330` found a largely continuous relationship rather than a magic threshold.

`SRC-0331` shows that listening can sometimes be adequately understood at lower lexical coverage than reading, with substantial learner variability.

Together with the earlier video-readiness work, the safe conclusion is:

```text
coverage = learner × material compatibility signal
coverage ≠ universal unlock rule
```

There is no defensible global rule:

```text
know N words
→ unlock reading + listening + video
```

## 5. Spoken and written English require different corpus evidence

`SRC-0341` demonstrates that academic spoken English has lexical distributions sufficiently distinct from written academic language to motivate a dedicated spoken list.

`SRC-0333` identifies frequent spoken collocations that single-word lists cannot represent.

The implication is general rather than academic-specific: if Nếp targets listening/conversation, the selection corpus must contain representative spoken interaction rather than relying on written corpora alone.

## 6. Multiword units are first-class content candidates

`SRC-0334` constructs a list of frequent nontransparent phrasal expressions.

`SRC-0335`, a meta-analysis of 35 studies / 130 effect sizes / 1,981 participants, finds a processing advantage for most multiword-sequence subtypes, moderated by properties such as phrasal frequency/association and task characteristics.

`SRC-0336` synthesizes collocation learning research.

This supports representing:

```text
single lexeme
multiword unit / collocation
```

as distinct candidate content types.

It does **not** imply that every frequent chunk should be directly memorized or taught productively.

## 7. Grammar content should include constructions, not only rule labels

`SRC-0337` provides usage-based evidence that construction learning is influenced by frequency, frequency distribution and function.

This complements RQ-012 and RQ-022: a construction is useful when it connects form to meaning/function across exemplars. Corpus data can help identify recurrent patterns and prototypes, but a corpus rank is not a grammar teaching order.

Relevant dimensions include:

- token frequency;
- type frequency / exemplar diversity;
- contingency;
- prototypicality;
- communicative function;
- target-task reuse.

## 8. Pragmatic routines must come from target discourse

`SRC-0338` shows how corpus evidence can be used to develop materials for pragmatic routines.

`SRC-0339` illustrates mismatch between self-study-book bundles and language useful in actual lingua-franca interaction.

Thus the content inventory must include interaction-organizing language such as clarification, repair, stance and conversational management when target discourse supports it.

## 9. Frequency, usefulness and learning cost are not the same variable

`SRC-0340` explicitly explores multi-criteria vocabulary selection rather than frequency alone.

RQ-019 adds Vietnamese-L1 evidence: population-level pronunciation, collocation, morphology and grammar difficulties can inform expected learning cost, but cannot diagnose an individual learner.

RQ-023 adds target-task importance.

Therefore candidate priority is multi-dimensional.

## 10. Provisional content-selection evidence model

RQ-027 supports the following **selection dimensions**, not final numeric weights:

```text
TARGET-TASK VALUE
+ PORTABLE GENERAL UTILITY
+ TOKEN FREQUENCY
+ RANGE / DISPERSION
+ MODALITY / REGISTER FIT
+ FORMULAIC / CONSTRUCTION VALUE
+ COVERAGE CONTRIBUTION
+ LEARNABILITY / L1 COST
+ GENERATIVE / PREREQUISITE VALUE
+ INDIVIDUAL LEARNER GAP
```

A candidate should also retain corpus provenance:

```text
corpus
subcorpus/domain
modality
register
sample period
counting unit
frequency
range/dispersion
association statistics if multiword
```

## 11. Strong exclusions

RQ-027 rejects these shortcuts:

```text
frequency rank = teaching order
CEFR vocabulary level = teaching order
word family known = every family member known
98% coverage = guaranteed comprehension
3,000 words = universal authentic-content gate
single-word inventory = complete lexical curriculum
native-speaker corpus = automatically correct target corpus
large corpus = automatically representative corpus
```

## 12. Target-population directness

Evidence directness is mixed.

Strong general corpus/vocabulary evidence exists, and `SRC-0328` plus earlier RQ-019 work supply some Vietnamese evidence. However, direct corpus and learning-cost evidence for **Vietnamese adults near Pre-A1 learning for general/workplace use** remains limited.

Therefore the exact first content set and weighting function remain hypotheses for `EXP-027` and subsequent curriculum validation.
