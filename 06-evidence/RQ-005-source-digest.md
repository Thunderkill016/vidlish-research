# RQ-005 source digest — changed-context transfer

## Research question

What changed-context probe is feasible enough to measure transfer repeatedly for a Vietnamese-speaking learner near A0 without turning every lesson into a long proficiency test?

## Working definitions

For this research cycle:

- **retention / same-task performance** = the cue/context/required response substantially reproduces what was trained;
- **transfer / generalisation evidence** = the learner succeeds when at least one meaningful cue, context, exemplar, surface form, modality or required response differs from training;
- **near transfer** = the target communicative relation remains stable and only a small number of dimensions change;
- **farther transfer** = several dimensions change or the learner must independently formulate a response.

These are measurement labels for Nếp, not claims that transfer is one unitary cognitive ability.

## Source set and extraction

### `SRC-0041` — Pan & Rickard, transfer of test-enhanced learning meta-analysis

- 192 transfer effect sizes from 122 experiments.
- Retrieval practice produced positive average transfer relative to non-testing re-exposure, but transfer was highly conditional on the kind of change between training and final test.
- Transfer was not equivalent to ordinary same-task retention.

**Use for Nếp:** a scheduled retrieval can help learning, but `transferred` requires an actual changed requirement. The product must record what changed.

### `SRC-0045` — Norman et al., contextual diversity and generalisation

- Adult word learners encountered pseudowords in contextually diverse or non-diverse sentence sets.
- Non-diverse learning supported familiar-context performance better.
- Diverse learning supported performance in unfamiliar, untrained contexts better.
- The transfer test used an unseen cloze context while keeping the target meaning and response space bounded.

**Scope limit:** L1/new-word learning rather than Vietnamese A0 L2 English. Strongly useful for transfer-test design, not an exact curriculum rule.

### `SRC-0046` — Chandy, Serrano & Pellicer-Sánchez, L2 contextual diversity

- Adult L2 English learners read repeated same versus different texts containing novel items.
- Same-text repetition produced faster processing and stronger short-term gains.
- Context variation increased processing difficulty and did not produce a general vocabulary-test advantage in this design.

**Use for Nếp:** easier/familiar-context success can overstate flexible knowledge, but contextual variation is not automatically beneficial. Do not make every transfer probe maximally novel.

### `SRC-0047` — Oikawa & Uchihara, L2 contextual diversity (2026)

- 124 Japanese learners of English encountered target words across higher or lower contextual diversity.
- Higher contextual diversity improved delayed meaning recognition overall.
- Prior vocabulary knowledge moderated the effect: learners with stronger vocabulary tended to benefit more, while highly varied input could be adverse for lower lexical proficiency.

**Use for Nếp:** near-A0 novelty must be bounded. Transfer difficulty should grow with demonstrated capability rather than being fixed globally.

### `SRC-0048` — Elgort et al., contextual learning of L2 word meanings

- Adult bilinguals learned rare L2 words from supportive sentence contexts and were tested a day later in old and new contexts.
- Newly learned semantic knowledge was stronger/more flexible for higher-proficiency learners.
- Familiar contexts could support more episodic processing; new congruous contexts were useful for probing semantic access beyond the original episode.

**Use for Nếp:** old-context performance and new-context performance support different inferences, and learner proficiency matters.

### `SRC-0049` — Pagán & Nation, learning words across varied contexts

- Adult readers learned unfamiliar words in same versus diverse sentence contexts.
- Diverse contexts slowed learning-phase processing but improved subsequent processing when words appeared in new neutral sentences.

**Scope limit:** L1 adult word learning, used as convergent indirect evidence for context independence and probe design.

### `SRC-0050` — Schneider, Healy & Bourne, foreign vocabulary retention and transfer

- French-English vocabulary training manipulated difficulty through translation direction and contextual interference.
- Some harder learning conditions reduced immediate performance but improved delayed retention and transfer across translation direction.

**Use for Nếp:** exact training performance is not a complete learning signal; changed response requirements can reveal knowledge that same-task practice misses.

### `SRC-0051` — Healy, Schneider & Kole (2025), difficulty, retention and transfer

- Re-examined several difficulty manipulations in foreign-vocabulary learning and added two experiments.
- Difficult conditions did not uniformly improve later outcomes; benefits depended on the manipulation and quality of initial learning.
- Some transfer effects were negative when prior semantic-category learning created interference.

**Use for Nếp:** “desirable difficulty” cannot justify arbitrary novelty. Difficulty must remain learnable and empirically calibrated.

### `SRC-0052` — Teng & Xu, receptive-to-productive vocabulary development

- Compared receptive and productive vocabulary exercises over repeated practice.
- Productive tasks produced stronger delayed productive outcomes than receptive tasks.
- Repetition helped, with diminishing gains after early repetitions.

**Use for Nếp:** a recognition-style transfer probe cannot certify productive ability. If the target product claim is production, at least some probes must require production.

### `SRC-0053` — Meara & Fitzpatrick, productive vocabulary assessment

- Reviews practical problems in measuring productive vocabulary.
- Controlled productive items can efficiently target specific words, especially at lower vocabulary levels, while free-production samples are broad but expensive and context-dependent.

**Use for Nếp:** near A0, constrained production can be a legitimate measurement stage; free conversation is not required to test every target.

## Evidence boundaries

This cycle did **not** establish:

- one universal number of context dimensions that must change;
- that more contextual diversity is always better;
- that a cloze task proves speaking ability;
- that a single sentence-generation task proves general language transfer;
- that far-transfer probes should be used on first-day A0 learning;
- a validated Vietnamese near-A0 transfer difficulty scale;
- how often transfer probes should occur in production without excessive testing burden.

Those product-specific thresholds belong in `EXP-005`.
