# RQ-007 source digest — Which pronunciation targets matter most for intelligibility?

## Scope

RQ-007 does **not** ask which features make Vietnamese-accented English sound most native-like. It asks a narrower product question:

> Which pronunciation properties should Nếp prioritize because they are plausibly costly to **actual understanding or ease of understanding**, especially for Vietnamese-speaking adults near A0?

The evidence set deliberately combines:

1. meta-analyses/reviews defining the intelligibility/comprehensibility target;
2. general L2 evidence about feature importance;
3. direct Vietnamese-English production/perception evidence;
4. explicit limitations where Vietnamese-specific causal evidence is missing.

## Evidence table

| Source | Population / design | Atomic contribution to RQ-007 | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `SRC-0062` Lee, Jang & Plonsky (2015) | meta-analysis; 86 pronunciation-instruction reports | Pronunciation instruction is generally effective; effects vary with intervention duration, feedback and outcome type. | Broad L2 evidence; does not prescribe Vietnamese target order or prove spontaneous communicative transfer. |
| `SRC-0063` Saito (2021) | two meta-analyses: listener studies + training studies | Comprehensibility and nativelikeness/accentedness depend on partly different information; instruction improves comprehensibility more readily than nativelikeness. | Most evidence is not Vietnamese-specific; listener ratings of comprehensibility are not identical to actual intelligibility. |
| `SRC-0064` Chau & Huensch (2025) | meta-analysis of 49 reports / 141 correlations | Accentedness is only weakly related to intelligibility relative to other FICA relations; a noticeable accent does not imply unintelligible speech. | Correlational synthesis; does not identify a Vietnamese curriculum. |
| `SRC-0065` Hu, Kuo & Dixon (2022/2025 issue) | meta-analytic review of intelligibility/comprehensibility interventions | Interventions can improve learner intelligibility; task and measurement choices moderate effects. | Broad English-learner population; no single feature ordering. |
| `SRC-0066` Suzukida & Saito (2019/2021) | two Japanese-EFL speech studies | High-functional-load consonant substitutions were more damaging to comprehensibility than low-functional-load substitutions. | Japanese population and comprehensibility ratings; functional load is a prioritization heuristic, not a Vietnamese phoneme whitelist. |
| `SRC-0067` Ghosh & Levis (2021) | L1/L2 listener word-identification experiments | Word-stress deviations are especially damaging when they also distort vowel quality; not every stress deviation is equally serious. | Controlled word-level tasks; not Vietnamese-specific. |
| `SRC-0068` Nguyễn & Ingram (2005) | Vietnamese learners of English; word-stress research | Vietnamese acquisition of English word stress is a genuine L1/L2 prosodic problem worth diagnosis. | Does not by itself establish the exact intelligibility cost or instructional priority of every stress deviation. |
| `SRC-0069` Nguyễn, Ingram & Pensalfini (2008) | Vietnamese beginner/advanced speakers; acoustic/perceptual prosody study | Vietnamese speakers can rely on L1-compatible pitch cues while struggling with English timing/deaccenting; advanced learners show adaptation. | Focuses contrastive stress patterns, not an A0 curriculum or direct priority ranking. |
| `SRC-0070` Dang (2018) | 50 Vietnamese adult EFL learners | Vietnamese syllable-structure transfer into English closed syllables/CVL patterns can compromise word shape and intelligibility. | One Vietnamese learner sample/context; should motivate diagnostics rather than stereotyping every learner. |
| `SRC-0071` Kang, Vo & Moran (2016) | 240 American, Vietnamese and Arabic listeners rating Vietnamese-accented English | Listener background changes how consonant, vowel and stress deviations affect judgments/understanding. | Manipulated stimuli and group-level results; no universal listener-neutral rank follows. |
| `SRC-0072` Tran & Yeh (2019) | 2 Vietnamese speakers, 8 Taiwanese EFL listeners + automated transcription | Convergent evidence implicates vowel quality/duration, final-sound/cluster loss and stress/intonation in difficult Vietnamese-accented speech. | Very small speaker/listener samples and scripted reading; useful only as preliminary/convergent evidence. |
| `SRC-0073` Disney & Le (2024) | 16 Vietnamese adults; about 25,000 usable words / 240 minutes naturalistic monologue | Word-final `/s,z/` omission is frequent; morphological endings and consonant-cluster environments carry substantially higher omission risk. | Focuses `/s,z/`, not all codas; observed error frequency does not independently prove causal intelligibility cost for every token. |
| `SRC-0074` Cunningham (2010) | Vietnamese-accented vowel production and listener-identification work | Some Vietnamese speakers collapse or weakly realize English vowel distinctions/quantity cues enough to create word-identification difficulty without contextual rescue. | Older/small samples and accent-model assumptions; use for candidate diagnostics, not blanket correction. |
| `SRC-0075` Dao, Nguyen & Nguyen (2021) | 72 intermediate Vietnamese EFL university learners; pronunciation instruction vs control | Teaching syllables/segments and later word stress/thought groups/sentence focus can interact with listening, but durable listening gains were not consistently maintained. | Intermediate rather than near-A0; listening outcome, not direct speech-intelligibility outcome. |
| `SRC-0076` Nguyễn & Đào (2018) | 20 Southern Vietnamese English speakers + 10 Australian controls | Beginning Vietnamese learners show L1-related question-intonation patterns; advanced speakers show adaptation with experience. | Demonstrates transfer/development, not that native-like question tunes deserve early priority for intelligibility. |

## Strongest convergent findings

### A. Accent reduction is the wrong optimization target

`SRC-0063` and `SRC-0064` separate **accentedness** from communicative success. A learner can remain noticeably Vietnamese-accented while becoming easier and more accurate to understand.

Product implication: never rank pronunciation work by “distance from a native accent” alone.

### B. Preserve information-bearing word shape before polishing accent detail

The most direct Vietnamese-specific evidence converges on English syllable/coda difficulty:

- Vietnamese-to-English syllable-structure transfer can weaken closed syllables and CVL forms (`SRC-0070`).
- final consonant and cluster loss appears repeatedly in Vietnamese-English intelligibility work (`SRC-0072`, `SRC-0074`);
- naturalistic 2024 data show substantial omission of final `/s,z/`, especially where morphology and clusters stack (`SRC-0073`).

This supports a **candidate early priority** on whether the listener receives the information-bearing coda/cluster at all, not whether every allophonic detail sounds native.

### C. Morphological endings deserve special attention when sound loss also removes grammar/meaning

`SRC-0073` shows markedly greater `/s,z/` omission risk for morphological endings and clusters. For Nếp, the pedagogical danger is not “accent” but disappearance of information such as plural/third-person/possessive/clitic cues when the task depends on them.

RQ-007 directly supports `/s,z/` as a high-value diagnostic family. It does **not** yet establish one global rule for every English inflectional ending; those require item- and feature-specific evidence.

### D. Use functional load to prioritize contrasts, but never as a universal phoneme list

General L2 work (`SRC-0066`) supports correcting high-information contrasts before low-information substitutions when time is limited. This means Nếp should not mechanically teach every IPA contrast or assume a visibly nonnative `/θ/` realization matters more than a contrast that repeatedly changes word identity.

Because `SRC-0066` is not Vietnamese-specific, functional load is a **weight in a priority model**, not a hard curriculum table.

### E. Lexical stress matters most when it damages lexical access

Vietnamese prosodic transfer is real (`SRC-0068`, `SRC-0069`), but `SRC-0067` provides an important severity rule: not all unexpected stress is equally harmful. Stress changes become more serious when they change vowel quality, remove a syllable or cause listeners to segment/identify the wrong word.

Product implication: teach **word identity preservation**, not “put stress exactly here because natives do.”

### F. Vowel work should be contrast-specific and diagnostic

`SRC-0074` gives direct reasons to test distinctions such as KIT–FLEECE and vowel-duration cues in Vietnamese-accented English. But English vowel inventories vary across legitimate accents, and listener use of cues varies.

Product implication: prioritize a vowel contrast only when:

1. it carries useful lexical information in the learner's target repertoire;
2. the learner actually collapses it;
3. listeners misidentify or work substantially harder because of it.

### G. Sentence stress, timing and intonation matter, but their early priority is conditional

General evidence (`SRC-0063`) supports prosodic instruction for comprehensibility, and Vietnamese research confirms transfer in stress/timing/intonation (`SRC-0069`, `SRC-0076`). `SRC-0075` also suggests pronunciation awareness can temporarily help listening.

This does **not** justify imposing a single “stress-timed native rhythm” or native intonation template on A0. Nếp should first use suprasegmentals where they carry communicative focus, word identity, chunk boundaries, question/interaction intent or materially reduce listener effort.

### H. The listener is part of intelligibility

`SRC-0071`, `SRC-0072` and the wider intelligibility literature show that listener language background and accent familiarity affect outcomes. Nếp therefore cannot validate a pronunciation feature only against one native-speaker model/listener group.

## What RQ-007 does NOT establish

The current literature does not establish:

- one fixed ordered list of English phonemes for every Vietnamese learner;
- that all Northern, Central and Southern Vietnamese speakers share the same errors;
- a universal threshold for “intelligible enough”;
- that `/θ, ð/`, aspiration, rhoticity or native-like final-stop release should be early priorities;
- that a native accent is the optimal model or product goal;
- that ASR word error rate is a valid proxy for human intelligibility;
- that one global pronunciation score is valid for near-A0 Vietnamese adults;
- the exact learner-time trade-off between coda, stress, vowel and prosodic interventions.

Those boundaries feed directly into `EXP-007` and RQ-008.
