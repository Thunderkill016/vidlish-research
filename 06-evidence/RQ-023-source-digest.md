# RQ-023 source digest — target needs and capability selection

**RQ:** What real-world English tasks/capabilities are highest-value for the target population, and how should current needs, target situations, learner goals and general-English portability determine the first curriculum?

**Status:** initial meta-foundation evidence digest  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Registry:** `data/sources-rq023.json`, `data/claims-rq023.json`

## 1. Why this RQ exists

The previous research program became good at asking **how to teach** before it had established **what the target learner most needs to be able to do**.

That creates a curriculum failure mode:

```text
CEFR list / word list / grammar list / textbook topics
→ arrange into units
→ call it a curriculum
```

RQ-023 reverses the dependency:

```text
target learner
→ target situations
→ target tasks
→ successful target discourse / artefacts
→ capability priorities
→ language/content selection
```

Needs analysis is not a substitute for SLA theory. It answers the `what`; RQ-022 constrains the `how`.

## 2. Methodological foundation

### SRC-0267 — Serafini, Lake & Long (2015)

Review and methodological analysis of needs-analysis practice for specialized learner populations.

Main value for Nếp:

- one stakeholder is not enough;
- one questionnaire is not enough;
- learner wants, expert judgments and observed target demands can diverge;
- multiple sources and methods increase defensibility;
- task-based needs analysis should identify what people actually need to accomplish in the target domain.

A product-owner intuition such as “Vietnamese beginners mainly need speaking” is therefore only a hypothesis.

### SRC-0268 — Serafini & Torres (2015)

A multiphase task-based needs analysis for a professional language course.

The study demonstrates a useful pattern:

```text
domain insiders generate target tasks
↓
learners rate task frequency / difficulty
↓
tasks are grouped into target-task types
↓
course objectives and pedagogic tasks are derived
```

This is much stronger than asking learners which grammar or topics they want.

### SRC-0269 — Long, target discourse

Identifying the target task is only the first half.

The second half is obtaining genuine samples of language used to perform the task and analyzing them.

For Nếp:

```text
“write a work email”
```

is still too vague.

We need examples of:
- why the email is sent;
- relationship between writer and recipient;
- typical information structure;
- recurring formulaic language;
- politeness/directness choices;
- acceptable variation;
- consequences of misunderstanding.

Only then should RQ-027 select vocabulary/chunks/constructions.

### SRC-0280 — recent TBNA systematic review

The review reports continued reliance on Long-style triangulation and mixed methods, while highlighting weaknesses such as poorly validated instruments and insufficient workplace observation.

Nếp should therefore treat a cheap online survey as **phase 1**, not as the complete needs analysis.

## 3. CEFR is useful here only as a map

### SRC-0270 — Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume

CEFR distinguishes language use across broad domains such as:

- personal;
- public;
- educational;
- professional;

and language activities such as reception, production, interaction and mediation.

This is useful for preventing domain blindness.

It does **not** answer:

```text
which task matters most to this Vietnamese adult?
```

or:

```text
what should Unit 1 teach?
```

RQ-023 uses CEFR as a coverage taxonomy, never as a priority algorithm.

## 4. What Vietnamese adult learners say they want

### SRC-0275 — 148 adult learners in five HCMC language centers

Reported motives included workplace/customer/colleague communication and qualification requirements, with smaller groups reporting study-abroad, job-seeking, entertainment and travel motives.

Important learning constraints included:
- low confidence;
- limited time;
- insufficient speaking/practice environment.

The sample also wanted more realistic communicative learning.

### SRC-0276 — adult EFL learners in Da Nang

Career progression, job requirements and communication again appeared as major motives.

Time and financial constraints and limited prior English were prominent difficulties.

### Inference boundary

These studies support:

```text
career + practical communication matter to many Vietnamese adults
```

They do not support:

```text
all Vietnamese adults need the same workplace syllabus
```

Motivation is not a target-task inventory.

## 5. Vietnamese workplace evidence

### SRC-0271 — professional engineers

Task-based needs analysis involving 291 engineers, 40 employers and 39 interviewees identified a large set of actual English workplace tasks.

Frequently relevant activities included practical/technical reading, professional email and other work communication. Employers and engineers did not always rate needs identically.

A substantial reported mismatch existed between university English courses and workplace needs.

The methodological lesson is as important as the task list:

```text
role
company type
professional area
```

changed task frequency.

### SRC-0272 — IT graduates/workplace communication

Qualitative observations and interviews found a gap between university English education and actual communication in an HCMC IT workplace.

Workplace competence included relational and pragmatic communication, not merely technical vocabulary and grammar.

### SRC-0273 — import/export services

Authentic workplace data included interviews, emails and phone calls.

A key finding was that workplace English as a lingua franca often prioritized:

```text
meaning / function / adaptability
```

over textbook-perfect lexicogrammatical form.

This does not make accuracy irrelevant. It means success criteria must fit the task and communicative stakes.

### SRC-0274 — mechanical engineers

Mixed methods included managers, engineers, interviews and workplace observation.

Real target situations included technical and commercial activity such as ordering parts, interpreting technical information and contract-related communication.

The research also surfaced:
- accent and lingua-franca variability;
- repair and adaptation;
- technical communication;
- social communication;
- power/relationship effects.

### SRC-0277 — tourism students

Learners reported a need for stronger communicative English and more practical use opportunities.

This supports practical alignment but remains a learner-perception study rather than direct observation of all tourism work.

### SRC-0278 — food & beverage employees

Workplace needs were highly tied to service sequence and authentic situations, including menu explanation, telephone communication and travel-related assistance.

The lesson for general curriculum design is not to copy hotel English into Nếp. It is that **task context changes language priority**.

### SRC-0279 — Business English stakeholders

Stakeholders emphasized listening, speaking and writing plus concrete workplace situations and authentic documents.

Business subdomains differed in their priorities.

Again:

```text
“Business English”
```

is itself too broad to be a single task domain.

## 6. What converges across Vietnamese contexts

The studies are heterogeneous, but recurring **task families** appear:

```text
understand spoken information / requests
respond or explain
ask for clarification / repair misunderstanding
read practical or technical information
exchange short written messages / email
confirm details
participate in calls / meetings / service interactions
adapt language to interlocutor and context
```

These are candidate high-portability families, not a final Nếp sequence.

## 7. What does not converge

There is no defensible universal ranking such as:

```text
1 speaking
2 listening
3 vocabulary
4 grammar
```

Different jobs produce different profiles.

Examples:
- hotel/frontline service can be heavily oral;
- engineering can require substantial reading of instructions/specifications plus oral coordination;
- import/export can involve both written digital genres and calls;
- business roles vary by function.

Therefore Nếp should model capability needs by **task and domain**, not by one global skill preference.

## 8. General-English portability problem

Nếp is not currently an ESP course for one occupation.

So RQ-023 needs two layers:

```text
PORTABLE CORE
capabilities useful across many domains
↓
DOMAIN BRANCHES
work / study / travel-public / digital / specific occupations
```

Candidate portable capability families to validate include:

1. identify and exchange basic personal/context information;
2. understand short requests, questions and instructions;
3. request clarification, repetition or confirmation;
4. exchange high-value details such as number, time, date, price, place and contact information;
5. make a simple request and respond to one;
6. read short practical messages, labels, instructions and interface text;
7. write a short functional message to request, confirm or update;
8. open, maintain and close a short social/transactional interaction;
9. cope with an unfamiliar word or misunderstanding without immediate conversational collapse.

**Status:** hypotheses from cross-domain overlap. They are not yet the first Nếp curriculum.

## 9. Direct Nếp needs study required

Published research cannot replace direct sampling of the intended learner population.

The study should collect at minimum:

### Learner profile
- current English evidence;
- age band;
- work/study status;
- occupation/domain if relevant;
- immediate English goal;
- likely English-use situations in the next 6–24 months;
- available study time;
- access to real English interaction.

### Target-task elicitation
Ask what the learner actually needs to do, for example:

```text
NOT: “Do you need speaking?”

BUT:
“When English becomes necessary at work, who are you speaking with, through what channel, and what are you trying to accomplish?”
```

### Task ratings
For each candidate task:
- frequency;
- importance / consequence;
- current difficulty;
- urgency;
- need for training.

### Triangulation
Where a domain matters:
- learner;
- recent successful worker/student;
- employer/domain expert/teacher;
- authentic text/audio/task sample where feasible.

## 10. Stop condition for RQ-023

RQ-023 can support `SYN-METHOD-001` when:

1. published needs evidence has been mapped;
2. target population heterogeneity is explicit;
3. portable-core hypotheses are separated from domain branches;
4. a direct Nếp target-needs study is specified;
5. exact first-curriculum priorities remain provisional until that direct study supplies data.
