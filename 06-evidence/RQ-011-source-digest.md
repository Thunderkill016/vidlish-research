# RQ-011 source digest

## Strongest/direct evidence

### SRC-0111 — Roldán-Mora & Cárdenas-Claros (2024)
Systematic review of technology-enhanced L2 playback controls. Learner control can help comprehension repair and strategy use, but use varies and training is often needed.

### SRC-0112 — Mirzaei & Meshgi (2021)
Computer-assisted L2 listening system automatically repeated/slowed predicted difficult segments. Promising local recognition results; limited evidence for long-term continuous transfer.

### SRC-0113 — Fang et al. (2021)
Artificial pause placement changed L2 sentence processing and interacted with proficiency. Important evidence that inserted pauses alter linguistic/prosodic processing rather than simply adding neutral time.

### SRC-0115 — Sheppard & Butler (2017)
Paused transcription provides useful diagnosis of what learners perceived, including segmentation and decoding problems.

### SRC-0117 — Blau (1990)
Classic L2 input-modification evidence that pauses can affect comprehension; relevant as background, not sufficient for modern adaptive auto-pause policy.

## Supporting / boundary evidence

### SRC-0114 — Liu, Lin & Kalyuga (2021)
General multimedia experiment: system and learner pausing both improved learning relative to continuous presentation under complex conditions. Not an L2 listening study, so use only for mechanism plausibility.

### SRC-0116 — Carney (2022)
Natural filled pauses/fillers had context-dependent effects in unscripted L2 listening, reinforcing that temporal interruptions interact with linguistic location and learner processing.

### SRC-0118 — Luu, Lian & Siriyothin (2021)
Vietnamese beginning EFL learners benefited from a self-regulated prosody-focused listening intervention. Relevant to learner agency/prosody, but auto-pause was not isolated.

## Main evidence gap

There is no strong direct literature establishing that an adaptive system which automatically pauses authentic English at predicted difficult points produces better **delayed unseen continuous listening** for Vietnamese near-A0 learners than learner-controlled replay or failure-contingent repair.

That gap belongs to `EXP-011`.
