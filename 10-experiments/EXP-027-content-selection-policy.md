# EXP-027 — Corpus-informed content selection policy validation

**Status:** planned  
**Depends on:** RQ-023, RQ-024, RQ-027  
**Population:** Vietnamese-speaking adults near Pre-A1/A1

## Question

Does a multi-criteria content selector produce a more useful and learnable beginner curriculum than frequency-only, CEFR/list-based or owner/AI-curated content selection?

## Candidate policies

### A — Frequency-only

Select items primarily by a modern general-English frequency list.

### B — CEFR/profile/list-based

Select from CEFR/English Vocabulary Profile/general-service materials using published level/list order.

### C — Target-needs + corpus

Select from target-task evidence plus representative spoken/written corpora using frequency + range/dispersion + modality/register fit.

### D — Multi-criteria selector

C plus:

- multiword/construction/pragmatic-unit evidence;
- learner gap evidence;
- Vietnamese-L1 learning-cost priors;
- coverage contribution;
- generative/prerequisite value;
- held-out corpus validation.

## Fixed exposure budget

All conditions must use the same approximate learning-time/content budget. Otherwise a larger curriculum can win by simply teaching more.

## Evaluation layers

### Layer 1 — held-out target-discourse utility

On target corpora not used to build the selector, measure:

- token/type coverage;
- target-task language coverage;
- range/dispersion of selected items;
- modality/register coverage;
- useful multiword/construction/routine coverage;
- redundancy / low-value inclusion.

### Layer 2 — expert/domain-insider judgment

Blind review of selected content for:

- target-task necessity;
- communicative usefulness;
- appropriateness/register;
- obvious omissions;
- obvious low-value inclusions.

Expert judgment is evidence, not ground truth; disagreement must be retained.

### Layer 3 — target-learner learning study

Learners receive matched learning time from content sets generated by the competing policies.

Measure:

- immediate form–meaning/function learning;
- delayed retention;
- changed-context recognition/use;
- target-task performance;
- learning minutes per retained useful item;
- proportion of deliberate-learning time spent on already-known or low-need content.

### Layer 4 — curriculum consequence

Track whether the selector improves downstream capability learning:

```text
retained + changed-context target-capability gain
───────────────────────────────────────────────
learning minutes
```

This remains the main educational criterion.

## Important subgroup checks

Analyze separately by:

- true near-A0 vs A1-ish learners;
- target-domain cluster;
- literacy/education background;
- learner goals;
- Vietnamese-L1 diagnostic evidence;
- spoken vs written target tasks.

## Failure conditions for policy D

The multi-criteria selector should be simplified or rejected if:

- it does not outperform simpler C on held-out target utility or learner outcomes;
- added dimensions mainly add complexity/noise;
- Vietnamese-L1 priors increase unnecessary remediation;
- generative-value scores fail to predict reusable learning;
- corpus-derived differences do not survive held-out corpora;
- content selection improves lexical coverage but not target-task learning/use.

## No premature optimization

Do not fit weights against the same corpus or learners used to define the dimensions.

Required separation:

```text
development corpus / sample
→ selector design

held-out corpus / learner sample
→ validation
```

## Decision criterion

Do not ship a universal weighted ranking merely because it correlates with frequency or expert preference.

Prefer the simplest policy that reliably improves:

```text
useful target-language coverage
+
retained / changed-context learning
+
target-task capability
```

without unnecessary curriculum complexity.
