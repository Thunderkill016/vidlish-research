# RQ-017 source digest

## Research question

How should Nếp sequence and integrate listening, vocabulary/chunks, constructions, pronunciation, speaking, reading, writing, interaction and fluency across beginner development without either siloing skills or overloading the learner?

## Course balance is a horizon-level property

### SRC-0007 — Nation, The Four Strands [existing registry]
Nation proposes four broad strands across a balanced course:

```text
meaning-focused input
meaning-focused output
language-focused learning
fluency development
```

The framework explicitly treats listening/reading as possible input, speaking/writing as possible output, deliberate language work as a separate learning condition, and fluency as work using largely familiar language. Its rough equal-time balance is a curriculum heuristic, not a validated per-lesson algorithm; Nation himself describes equal allocation as arbitrary and discusses why all strands can exist from early stages.

Product implication: Nếp should audit balance across a multi-session horizon rather than force every lesson to contain every skill or allocate exactly 25% in each individual session.

## Organize around communicative capability, not isolated four-skill tabs

### SRC-0001 — CEFR Companion Volume [existing registry]
The CEFR descriptive scheme separates reception, production, interaction and mediation while also describing linguistic, sociolinguistic and pragmatic competence. Its action-oriented approach works backwards from what learners need to do in real situations and uses tasks/scenarios that can naturally combine several communicative activities.

Critical limitation: CEFR is descriptive and does not prescribe one teaching sequence or learning algorithm.

Product implication: use a small practical `canDo`/scenario as an organizing target, then select the minimum input, vocabulary/chunks, construction, pronunciation and output work needed to enable it.

## Tasks can organize integrated use, but TBLT effect claims need caution

### SRC-0183 — Bryfonski & McKay (2019; online 2017)
Meta-analysis of long-term task-based implementations reported positive overall effects across diverse L2 outcomes and highlighted implementation variables such as needs analysis and program cycles.

Useful implication: communicative tasks can organize meaningful language use over programs, not just as one-off speaking activities.

### SRC-0184 — Xuan, Cheung & Liu (2022; later issue publication)
Methodological reanalysis challenged the earlier meta-analysis's inclusion and effect-size choices. Their narrower synthesis still favored programs using tasks, but with a more modest aggregate effect and with substantial heterogeneity.

### SRC-0185 — Boers & Faez (2023)
Critical review argues the comparative TBLT literature is not mature enough to support a clean universal estimate because primary studies and reviews differ in what they call a `task`, `task-based` or `task-supported` program.

Combined decision:

```text
tasks are a promising integration frame
≠
TBLT is proven universally superior
```

Nếp should use meaningful tasks because they match the product's capability/transfer model, while testing the particular sequencing used in the product instead of citing a global TBLT effect as proof.

## Sequence task demands rather than simply add more language

### SRC-0186 — Abdi Tabari, Johnson & Farahanynia (2024)
Research synthesis/meta-analysis on task sequencing found evidence favoring simple-to-complex sequencing for some production outcomes, especially syntactic complexity and accuracy, but results depend on task design and operationalization.

### SRC-0187 — Jin & Yan (2025; online 2024)
Systematic review found partial support for task-complexity/sequencing models and reported an overall tendency for simple-to-complex sequencing to improve complexity, accuracy and fluency measures. The evidence base, however, is dominated by adult English learners and production tasks, especially writing, with heterogeneous operationalizations.

### SRC-0189 — Johnson (2017)
Meta-analysis of cognitive task complexity in L2 writing shows changing task complexity can alter complexity, accuracy and fluency differently rather than simply make all performance dimensions better at once.

Product implication:
- difficulty must be decomposed;
- raising one demand may create trade-offs;
- progression should increase a small number of dimensions at a time;
- `harder` should not mean simultaneously more unknown vocabulary, faster input, less support, longer output and a new interaction pattern.

## Planning/scaffolding can make output feasible

### SRC-0188 — Johnson & Abdi Tabari (2022)
Research synthesis/meta-analysis found clear effects of task planning on subsequent L2 oral production, although effects vary across complexity, accuracy, lexical complexity and fluency measures.

Product implication: before asking a beginner to produce language, Nếp can legitimately provide bounded preparation such as:
- comprehensible model/input;
- known useful chunks;
- scenario context;
- short planning/rehearsal;
- constrained choice.

Support must be provenance-tracked so supported performance is not misclassified as independent ability.

## Comprehension and production are complementary

### SRC-0123 — Shintani, Li & Ellis (2013) [existing registry]
Meta-analysis comparing comprehension-based and production-based grammar instruction found both approaches effective for receptive and productive knowledge. Comprehension-based instruction showed an early advantage on receptive knowledge, while production-based instruction showed a delayed advantage on productive knowledge.

This does not prove a universal `input first, output later` sequence for all skills. It does support the safer beginner design:

```text
make meaning processable
→ strengthen form-meaning mapping
→ require constrained production
→ expand production/interaction
```

rather than forcing unsupported free production immediately or delaying production indefinitely.

## Existing Nếp evidence already supplies modality-specific sequencing rules

RQ-017 must reuse—not replace—the narrower research cycles:

- `RQ-001`: word/chunk evidence;
- `RQ-002`: Vietnamese scaffold fading;
- `RQ-003`: listening perception vs meaning;
- `RQ-004`: delayed retrieval;
- `RQ-005`: changed-context transfer;
- `RQ-006`: guided speaking;
- `RQ-007`: intelligibility-first pronunciation;
- `RQ-012`: construction form-meaning-use;
- `RQ-013`: connected reading progression;
- `RQ-014`: writing progression;
- `RQ-015`: interaction/pragmatics;
- `RQ-016`: fluency/automaticity.

The integrated curriculum must compose these engines around a common capability without discarding their separate evidence dimensions.

## Critical distinction: integrated task ≠ integrated mastery score

One scenario may combine several activities:

```text
listen to a café question
→ understand choice
→ notice/retrieve “I'd like…”
→ say an order
→ read a short menu/message
→ respond to a follow-up turn
```

But the Evidence Engine should still store distinct evidence:

```text
listening comprehension
construction mapping
spoken retrieval
pronunciation/intelligibility
reading comprehension
interaction contingency
```

A learner can succeed on one dimension and fail another. Integration should improve ecological use, not hide diagnostics.

## Critical distinction: comprehensive over time ≠ everything at once

The evidence supports balance and meaningful integration at course/program level, but not a requirement that every beginner session simultaneously train:
- listening;
- speaking;
- reading;
- writing;
- grammar;
- vocabulary;
- pronunciation;
- interaction;
- fluency.

For near-A0, a better rule is:

```text
one communicative capability
+ one primary learning bottleneck
+ only the supporting modalities needed now
```

Then revisit the same capability through other modes over later sessions.

## Candidate complexity dimensions

Nếp should log at least:
- number/proportion of unknown lexical items;
- construction novelty;
- input speed/length;
- number of propositions/elements;
- reasoning/inference demand;
- number of interaction turns;
- partner/voice variability;
- output length;
- support level;
- time pressure;
- context novelty;
- modality switch count.

Changing several at once makes both learning and experimentation difficult to interpret.

## Candidate integrated capability cycle

```text
1. define useful CAN-DO / scenario
2. check prerequisites
3. give bounded comprehensible input
4. require meaning attempt
5. notice/strengthen target chunk/construction/sound
6. retrieve with reduced support
7. perform guided output or interaction
8. change one context/content dimension
9. record separate evidence by capability × modality
10. return after delay
11. later expand to another modality / harder task
```

Not every target requires all eleven phases in a single session.

## Evidence gaps

The literature does not establish for Vietnamese adults near A0:
- the exact order of communicative capabilities;
- exact skill/modality ratios by stage;
- exact number of modalities per session;
- the best beginner session duration;
- exact complexity increments;
- when to add reading/writing to a listening-speaking capability;
- when to move from controlled production to interaction;
- whether a task-first or model-first ordering works better for each capability;
- the best horizon for Four-Strands balance auditing;
- how much learner choice should alter sequencing;
- how prerequisites should be inferred from sparse evidence.

These belong to `EXP-017` rather than being hard-coded as scientific facts.
