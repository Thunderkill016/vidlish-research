# RQ-019 source digest — Vietnamese-specific calibration

**Evidence ID:** `EVD-RQ-019`  
**Question:** How should Nếp use evidence about Vietnamese L1 learners to calibrate diagnostics and instruction without turning population tendencies into stereotypes?

## Scope

This pass retained 13 sources that directly study Vietnamese learners or Vietnamese EFL input. It emphasizes learner data over contrastive prediction alone.

The evidence is uneven. Pronunciation/prosody and vocabulary/collocation have several strong direct studies. Listening, beginner grammar and pragmatics have thinner samples. True near-A0 Vietnamese adults are underrepresented across the literature.

## Convergence

### 1. Phonology is not one problem

The strongest Vietnamese-specific pattern is not simply “bad pronunciation.” Evidence separates:

- segment/coda/cluster structure,
- prominence and timing,
- perception versus production,
- task effects,
- learnability differences.

For Nếp this argues against a single pronunciation score or a fixed national error checklist.

### 2. Listening can fail before meaning

`SRC-0200` shows chunk transcriptions failing through sound-level operations. `SRC-0197` shows L1-sensitive cue weighting in perception. Together they support an explicit diagnostic branch:

```text
written form known
+ meaning known
+ audio failed
→ test phonological decoding / prominence / chunk boundary
→ only then decide whether vocabulary is the problem
```

### 3. Single words are not enough

`SRC-0201` and `SRC-0202` converge on weak formulaic knowledge relative to individual words. `SRC-0203` shows that collocation learning depends partly on prior vocabulary and cross-language congruency.

The product implication is not “teach idioms early.” It is to make high-utility chunks and collocations first-class targets and record their evidence independently.

### 4. Small morphology can carry large meaning

Vietnamese and English package grammatical meaning differently. Direct studies support attention to:

- articles,
- plural morphology / mass-count mapping,
- tense/aspect and agreement risks.

But the evidence does **not** justify building an A0 grammar course from error percentages in university writing. Nếp must verify these risks in the learner’s actual listening/speaking/reading/writing task.

### 5. Culture is context, not a fixed learner trait

The pragmatic source shows possible transfer in requests, but the sample is too small to justify a national stereotype. The safe implication is scenario-based pragmatic variation with role, distance, age and imposition represented explicitly.

## What is rejected

- “All Vietnamese learners delete final consonants.” — rejected.
- “Vietnamese nationality is enough to choose a pronunciation curriculum.” — rejected.
- “If Vietnamese and English structures differ, the learner must have that error.” — rejected.
- “Written grammar error frequency equals spoken beginner priority.” — rejected.
- “Knowing the words in a phrase means knowing the phrase.” — rejected.
- “Vietnamese learners have one politeness style.” — rejected.

## Evidence gap that matters most

The literature rarely samples the actual Nếp target: Vietnamese-speaking adults at true A0 / very low A1 doing short mobile tasks with independent delayed evidence.

Therefore RQ-019 can set **priors and diagnostic candidates**, not a final Vietnamese curriculum. That is the job of `EXP-019`.
