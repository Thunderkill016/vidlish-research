# Product principles

| ID | Principle | Confidence | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PRN-001` | Measure capability, not completion. | high | Learning claims require task evidence. |
| `PRN-002` | Keep comprehension, recall, transfer and retention separate. | high | They are different performances. |
| `PRN-003` | Attempt before reveal when practicing retrieval. | high | Visible answers convert retrieval into recognition/review. |
| `PRN-004` | Use bounded, understandable input before broad free output near A0. | moderate | Beginners need language to work with; controlled output can still begin early. |
| `PRN-005` | Space review, but do not equate scheduler state with mastery. | high | Spacing supports memory; schedule metadata is not a performance. |
| `PRN-006` | Treat captions and Vietnamese translation as scaffolds that can be faded. | moderate | Support can aid comprehension but can also replace the intended task. |
| `PRN-007` | Prefer intelligibility over native-accent imitation. | moderate | Product goal is successful communication, not accent conformity. |
| `PRN-008` | AI proposes; evidence and product gates decide. | moderate | Generation reliability and assessment validity are separate problems. |
| `PRN-009` | Authentic video is a graduated source, not proof of a beginner curriculum. | moderate | Difficulty and coverage must match learner capability. |
| `PRN-010` | Product assumptions need experiments even when mechanisms are plausible. | high | Educational evidence does not determine exact UX. |
